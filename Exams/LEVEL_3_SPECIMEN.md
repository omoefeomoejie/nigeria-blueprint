# Level 3 — Can You Defend It

*Written. Marked by an examiner who is not a member of this movement and is
paid whether candidates pass or fail.*
**Pass mark: 50%.** Three questions. Answer all three.

---

## 3.1 — The unseen claim
*(Specimen. The live paper uses a different, current claim.)*

> **"Our state has digitised its land registry. Titling is now fast and
> transparent."** — a state commissioner, in a press briefing.

**(a)** What confidence level would you place on this claim, and why?

**(b)** Who produced this claim, and whose interest does it serve?

**(c)** Name the **single fact** that would settle it — one number that, if
published, would make the claim checkable.

**(d)** Explain why that number does not currently exist anywhere in Nigeria.

*What a strong answer contains: recognition that the claim is unverifiable
rather than false; that the speaker is describing his own performance; that
"days from application to title, published monthly" is the settling fact; and
that no independent body counts land registry performance anywhere in the
country — which is exactly why the claim can be made safely.*

---

## 3.2 — The design question

> A governor announces a free school meals programme, funded from the state
> budget, run by a unit reporting directly to his office.

**(a)** Will it survive his successor? Give your reasoning.

**(b)** Who keeps the score on whether it is working?

**(c)** What three changes would you make so that it outlasts him — and what
would each one cost politically?

*What a strong answer contains: recognition that a unit reporting to the
governor's office is a gateman; that nobody independent is counting meals
served or children fed; and three fixes along the lines of statutory footing,
an independent count published on a fixed date, and an organised constituency
(parents' associations) who lose something real if it stops.*

---

## 3.3 — The adversarial question

> **State the strongest argument against this movement's central finding —
> that Nigeria's failures track its missing scorekeepers — and say what
> evidence would change your mind.**

**This question cannot be passed by agreeing with us.**

*What a strong answer might argue: that our evidence base was assembled to
answer a domestic question and would look different if we had spent sixteen
investigations on trade rules and currency; that publishing numbers has not
by itself fixed anything measurable in Nigeria, since no state can yet show
what transparency saved; that Kaduna scores 39/39 on transparency and is not
obviously better governed than states that score less; or that counting
requires a state capable of counting, so the finding may be circular.*

*And a strong answer names what would change its mind — for example: a state
that published everything for five years and got measurably worse, or a state
with no external scorer that delivered consistently anyway.*

**Marking note:** a candidate who cannot construct a serious argument against
us has not understood us, however well they have memorised us.
