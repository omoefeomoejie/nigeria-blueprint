# Mistakes.md — Where Intellectual Humility Becomes Institutional Memory

The most valuable file in the repository. Every investigation logs what fooled
us, so no future node is fooled the same way. Entries are never deleted; a
reversal is a permanent part of the record.

Each entry answers: What did we believe? What was wrong? Why were we fooled?
What changed our mind? What rule did it add?

---

## Node 1 — Revenue Structure

### Mistake 1.1 — We assumed Lagos demonstrated a transferable mechanism
- **Believed:** Lagos's IGR success proved a tax-reform mechanism other states
  could copy. The whole thread circled Lagos as *the* proof case.
- **Wrong because:** Lagos's outcome is confounded by uncopyable economic
  geography — a third of national GDP, the ports, the corporate HQs. Reform and
  endowment cannot be separated from the outside.
- **Why fooled:** The reform is *real* (LIRS, digital tax authority), which made
  it easy to credit the reform for an outcome the endowment also explains. A true
  cause distracted us from a second true cause.
- **Changed mind:** Comparing Lagos's IGR (₦815bn) to Taraba's (₦10.87bn) made the
  endowment impossible to ignore.
- **Rule added:** Comparative Statecraft Engine, Rule 1 — *Beware confounded
  success stories.*

### Mistake 1.2 — We treated "IGR growth" as one phenomenon
- **Believed:** Rising IGR was, by itself, the good outcome we were hunting.
- **Wrong because:** At least three different things share that label, and two of
  them are not the mechanism we want.
- **Why fooled:** A single number with a single name (IGR) hid three different
  causal stories.
- **Changed mind:** An independent analysis classified states by *type* of growth —
  automation (Enugu/Kaduna) vs. extractive (Rivers/Delta) vs. optics (Ekiti/Taraba).
- **Rule added:** Comparative Statecraft Engine, Rule 2 — *Not all IGR growth is the
  same phenomenon.* Also seeded the Evidence Standard's "optics" caution: revenue
  that doesn't convert to capital execution is a vanity metric.

### Mistake 1.3 — We nearly certified Osun on interested sources
- **Believed:** Osun looked like the clean, geography-free proof of the mechanism.
- **Wrong because (procedurally):** The mechanism story came only from a
  gubernatorial aide and an election-framed op-ed. We almost logged it as proven.
- **Why fooled:** The data was genuine (NBS), and genuine data lends false
  credibility to the *interpretation* attached to it.
- **Changed mind:** Noticing every "why it grew" source had a stake in the 2026
  Osun election.
- **Rule added:** Comparative Statecraft Engine, Rule 3 — *Prefer independent audits
  over interested claims.* Osun logged **Contested**, not Certified.

### Meta-mistake 1.0 — The project itself ran theory ahead of evidence
- **Believed:** Building more architecture (skills, laws, nodes) was progress.
- **Wrong because:** It produced a "well-governed empty warehouse" — elegant
  structure, zero falsifiable claims.
- **Changed mind:** One search produced more durable insight (how the project
  should work) than weeks of framework-building had.
- **Rule added:** Law Zero (evidence-first; no rule without a case) and the
  Investigation SOP's "pull real data early" step.

## Node 1 — Revenue Structure (Enugu certification)

### Mistake 1.4 — We filed Enugu as "automation broadened the tax base"
- **Believed:** Enugu's 4.8× IGR jump was the clean, transferable
  automation/compliance mechanism (tax-net widening via digitisation).
- **Wrong because:** Decomposition showed the growth was overwhelmingly *non-tax*.
  2024: ₦30bn tax vs ₦150bn non-tax. 2025: tax 12.6% vs non-tax 87.4%. The driver
  was asset recovery and dormant-asset revitalisation, not tax broadening.
- **Why fooled:** A single intermediary source ("automation and compliance") plus
  an aggregate figure ("IGR 4.8×") matched our hypothesis, so we didn't decompose.
- **Changed mind:** The ESIRS breakdown (corroborated across Vanguard, Daily Trust,
  Nairametrics, and BudgIT 2025) split the number by source.
- **Rule added:** Comparative Statecraft Engine, Rule 2.1 — *Decompose revenue by
  source before classifying.* Hypothesis "automation primarily drove Enugu" →
  **Rejected.**

### Mistake 1.5 — We almost over-corrected into "non-tax growth is suspect"
- **Believed (briefly):** Having found Enugu's growth was non-tax, the fix was a
  rule treating non-tax-heavy IGR growth with the same suspicion as resource rents.
- **Wrong because:** "Non-tax revenue" is not one mechanism — it spans recurring
  commercial income, dividends, licences, land-admin reform, one-off asset sales,
  royalties, recoveries. Some transferable, some not. Calling it all "suspect"
  pre-judges exactly what decomposition is meant to examine.
- **Why fooled:** A clean falsification creates momentum to swing to the opposite
  verdict. The over-correction *felt* like rigor.
- **Changed mind:** Review applied the Fact/Interpretation split to the proposed
  rule and exposed it as a verdict wearing a rule's clothing.
- **Rule added:** none — and that is the lesson. Rule 2.1 forces *decomposition*,
  not suspicion. A rejected rule is logged here so the over-correction isn't
  re-attempted.

### Note — what Enugu certified (not a mistake, recorded for symmetry)
Rule 4 was *earned*, not corrected into: Lagos and Enugu have opposite, both-
confounded revenue sources but share three transferable institutional layers
(collection integrity, fiscal management, execution). That decoupling of
"transferable mechanism" from "revenue source" is Node 1's most durable output.
