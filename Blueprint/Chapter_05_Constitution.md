# Chapter 5 — The Constitution: Which Doors Are Open

*We neither worship this Constitution nor demand its destruction. We put it
on trial as a system, and follow the evidence about which parts can move,
how, and in what order.*

---

## 1. Desired Outcome
By year 10: revenue-formula review is time-bound and mandatory rather than
discretionary; at least three further burden-shedding devolutions completed;
and local-government financial autonomy implemented in practice, not merely
declared.

## 2. Structural Matches
- **India** [Certified]: devolution moved 32% → 42%; GST Council created by
  amendment (states hold two-thirds of votes); centrally-sponsored schemes
  restructured 75:25 → 50:50 — repeated fiscal-settlement reform **without
  rupture**, in a large, poor, diverse federation.
- **Nigeria's own three paired cases, 2022–2024** [Certified]: the revenue
  formula died untransmitted; electricity devolution passed every gate; LG
  autonomy won in court and is defied in practice.

## 3. The Mechanism
India did not constitutionalize *percentages*; it constitutionalized a
**renegotiation process** — a five-yearly commission whose report must be
tabled. Nigeria has the commission (RMAFC) and lacks the mandatory tabling:
the 2022 proposal (federal share 52.68% → 45.17%) was simply never
transmitted [Certified]. **Our defect is not a frozen number; it is a
beneficiary-held gate.**

## 4. Preconditions (Rule 5)
India's acceptance rests on convention that Nigeria lacks. Any transplant
must therefore be *legal*, not conventional — a deadline in the text, not a
norm in the culture.

## 5. Nigerian Constraints — the gate map (Rule 9)
| Route | Gate-holder | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue formula | President (transmission) | …the centre isn't the loser — i.e. rarely |
| Constitutional amendment | NASS + 24 state assemblies | …the change sheds a burden (electricity, 2023) |
| Local-government autonomy | Governors (via assemblies) | …ratification coalitions mature (0 states 2015 → short by six in 2023) |
| Courts | Judiciary | …never for disbursement: rulings declare, losers wait |

## 6. Nigerian Adaptation — the sequenced route map
**(a) Take the open doors now — and build the capacity to use them first.**
Every burden-shedding devolution (power, rail, corrections) is politically
cheap for the centre. But cheap to acquire is not the same as ready to use:
16 states took electricity oversight by early 2026 and several produced
non-functional regulators and licences issued outside the legal framework,
with no measurable supply improvement [Certified — Sector Brief 02;
Mistakes 8.1]. **Sequence: build the regulator, then take the power, then
publish the baseline.**
**(b) Build the coalitions for the locked doors.** The LG trajectory shows
ratification support can be moved over ~a decade, with state legislators
increasingly voting against their governors [Certified].
**(c) The keystone:** amend s.162(2) to impose a transmission deadline on
the President and institutionalise five-yearly review — the Indian
mechanism, in law rather than convention. RMAFC has itself formally
requested this.
**(d) Never rely on a court alone:** pair every ruling with the enabling
amendment and an organized beneficiary constituency.

## 7. Political Economy
The keystone's gate-holder is the person it constrains. Honest answer: this
passes in a narrow window — a final-term president seeking legacy, a
reform-coalition NASS, or under sustained public pressure from a states'
alliance. Naming the difficulty is not defeatism; pretending it away is how
previous attempts died four times.

## 8. Early-Win Pilot
Not a state pilot — a **coalition pilot**: publish the gate map and the
2022 non-transmission record; convene willing states around the keystone
amendment; secure formal endorsements from ≥ 12 state assemblies before any
national push. Ratification arithmetic is built, not begged.

## 9. Scale Strategy
Sequence: open doors → coalition → keystone. Each success lowers the cost
of the next; each unit that grows stronger under devolution becomes a
louder voice for the money reforms.

## 10. Success Metrics
Year 1: gate map published; ≥ 6 assemblies endorsing. Year 3: ≥ 12
endorsements; ≥ 1 further devolution passed. Year 10: Outcome (§1) met.

## 11. Kill Condition
If after two full electoral cycles the keystone has no presidential or
legislative sponsor and endorsements stall below 12, the centre-route is
judged closed for this period — resources shift entirely to the units-first
track (Chapters 1–4, 6) and the constitutional question is revisited with
new evidence.

## Irreversibility Test (Rule 7)
**Stock:** amendments passed (electricity is already permanent — no one is
re-centralising it). **Flow:** the review process itself, which is why the
keystone matters more than any single formula: a mandatory process
renegotiates forever; a favourable number lasts one bargain.

### Evidence note (v0.15 — live test, 15 Aug 2026)
The 2025–26 RMAFC review is complete and its proposals are "ready for
transmission." Not transmitted. In the same period the commission's
*remuneration* proposals reached the President and became law. The gate is
observable in real time, sorted by loss-type, which is why §6(c)'s keystone
(a transmission deadline in s.162(2)) is this chapter's central ask rather
than a better formula. [Certified — contemporaneous reporting]
