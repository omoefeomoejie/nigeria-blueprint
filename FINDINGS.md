## The findings that changed what we believe

### 1. The map of Nigeria's failures is close to the map of its missing scorekeepers
Sixteen domains tested. Five failed, and in every one the state did the
thing *and* reported on the thing. Eleven passed, and in every one somebody
outside counted.

Consequences: competitive federalism is a property of measurement, not of
devolution; the first question about any reform is who will count it; and
founding a scorer where none exists may beat funding a programme inside it.
Nigeria has no independent auditor for land registries, state electricity
regulators, or whether a given borehole still works — the three domains that
failed hardest.
**[Domain verdicts Certified; the pattern is our inference]** ·
`Research Engine/SCOREKEEPER_TEST.md`, Part II Layer 4b

### 2. The state often cannot absorb what it already holds
Sixteen states took electricity oversight and could not operate it. ₦332bn
of education money sits unaccessed while 20 million children are out of
school. Water utilities produce in 28 states and function in 16.

Capacity is not a precondition to assume. It is the reform.
**[Certified]** · Part II Layer 3, Sectors 02/04/05

### 3. Absorption is organisational, not cultural or regional
UBEC completes 78% of its projects. The Ministry of Niger Delta Affairs
completes 16%. Same country, same contractors, same law, same year — 62
points apart. It is not culture, because culture is constant across those
rows. It is not region, because the spread is widest inside one government.
**[Certified — BudgIT Tracka]** · `Blueprint/ABSORPTION_PLAYBOOK.md`

### 4. The difference between a 65% agency and a 16% agency is when the money moves
REA pays only after delivery is verified. NDDC paid on award, and 12,000 of
13,377 projects were abandoned after payment, with commission staff taking
the larger share of contractor payments.

You cannot abandon a project after payment if payment arrives after
delivery. **This is the cheapest structural fix in the corpus**, and it
requires no better officials.
**[Certified]** · `Blueprint/ABSORPTION_PLAYBOOK.md`

### 5. Reforms in Nigeria are not defeated. They are outwaited.
Kaduna's teacher testing survived a strike, a lawsuit, and sacking the
union's national president — then stopped the month the governor left. In
the same state, at the same handover, the open-government machinery survived
and expanded. The difference was statutory housing and an organised outside
constituency.
**[Certified]** · Nodes 2 and 4, Rule 7

### 6. The transferable part of a fiscal success is never the revenue source
Lagos has ports. Enugu had recoverable assets. Neither transfers. What
transfers is the three layers underneath: block the leak, plan the money,
show the work. Replicated on three different revenue bases — Enugu, Kwara,
Osun.
**[Certified]** · Node 1, Chapter 1

### 7. Constitutional doors open by loss-type, not by merit
The revenue formula has not moved since 1992 because every proposal must
pass the desk of the tier that loses; a full proposal reached the President
in 2022 and was never transmitted. Electricity devolution passed every gate
in 2023 — because it shed a burden rather than moving money.
**[Certified]** · Node 5, Chapter 5

### 8. Identity in Nigeria is switched on, not inherited
Ethnic self-identification moved twenty points in eighteen months, rises at
elections, and hardens when the state discriminates. And Nigeria's own youth
service scheme measurably reduces ethnic bias, with effects lasting years —
the strongest causal evidence in this book, and it is domestic.
**[Certified — Afrobarometer; Okunogbe 2024, AEJ:Applied]** · Node 6

### 9. $300 billion is frozen behind a registration desk
More than 90% of Nigerian land is untitled. 71.4% of landlords hold no
document. Registration costs 15–30% of a property's value. Roughly 90% of
Nigerian houses were built from the owners' own savings.

The only sector where nothing needs building. States tax the doorway instead
of the room.
**[Certified]** · Sector 07

### 10. The security–agriculture loop
Insecurity closes 2.1 million hectares of farmland; closed farmland idles
young men; idle young men are vulnerable to recruitment; insecurity deepens.
FAO puts food production down 15%. Neither security nor livelihood work
finishes the job alone.
**[Certified — NAERLS, FAO, Northern Governors Forum]** · Sector 01,
Chapter 3, `Sectors/10_Interdependence_Map.md`

## The findings that survived an attempt to kill them

### 11. An ordinary state can escape federal dependence — with caveats we found ourselves
Enugu did it. But its debt more than doubled while it did, and its 2024
audited accounts are unpublished. Revenue autonomy part-financed by
borrowing is a different achievement from revenue autonomy.
**[Certified, with three qualifications from our own audit]** ·
Node 1 v1.5

### 12. Transparency works, and Nigeria has already proved it
NPHCDA published its health dashboard; an independent watchdog immediately
used the data to show 7.3% of a four-year target delivered. Twenty states
now receive money conditional on publishing records.
**[Certified]** · Sector 03, Node 4, Chapter 4

## What we got wrong, and corrected in public

- **Lagos** was our first proof case. It is confounded by geography — a
  seaport and a third of the national economy. Demoted.
- **Enugu's growth** was filed as automation broadening the tax base. It was
  87% non-tax asset recovery. Rejected and rewritten.
- **Vietnam** was our education model. At most 30% of its performance is
  explained by policy, most of that household inputs. Confounded. *(Second
  flagship-case trap: fame follows outcomes, not mechanisms.)*
- **"Take every open constitutional door"** was our advice until sixteen
  states took one and could not operate it. Corrected to: take the door and
  build the capacity first.
- **"Seventeen states abandon nothing"** was too clean. Only Cross River.
- **₦5,000 recruitment price** carried a tidy design conclusion for one
  commit, failed corroboration, and was downgraded. A figure that makes an
  argument neat deserves more scrutiny, not less.
- **Every revenue figure was nominal** across a period when prices doubled.
  Deflated, Osun's growth vanishes and Enugu's halves. We ran sixteen
  investigations before applying a CPI table.

Eleven entries, three correcting our own prior nodes. All permanent.
`Research Engine/Lessons Learned/Mistakes.md`

## What we still do not know

- No Nigerian selection reform has ever been shown to improve services.
- No measured Nigerian leakage-reduction figure exists. Searched three
  times. It has to be built, not found.
- Nobody has published what a bandit foot soldier earns from a ransom share
  — the number a livelihood programme must actually compete with.
- Whether pay-on-verified-output survives without donor-funded verifiers.
- Whether the Wager is true: that when enough people know the same thing and
  move, the country moves.

### 13. Money is not the binding constraint anywhere in this book
All eight chapters costed. The four a governor can start alone total roughly
**₦1.1bn–₦4.1bn** of capital — about 0.3% of Lagos's annual IGR, 6% of
Kaduna's, less than a single flyover.

The expensive items are **recurring, not capital**: Chapter 3's salaries and
Chapter 7's annual assessment. Which maps exactly onto Rule 7 — capital buys
stock, recurring buys flow. A state that funds the platform and not the
audit has bought the half that survives by default and skipped the half that
dies at succession.

**And the cheapest chapter is the hardest.** Chapter 5 costs almost nothing
and has failed five times since 2001.
**[Order-of-magnitude inference, anchored where Nigerian figures exist]** ·
`Blueprint/COSTING.md`

### 13a. The revenue pilot costs ₦250m–₦1bn and one political fight
Lagos budgets ₦250m for a digital tax platform against a ₦200bn target —
0.02% of its IGR, 0.35% of Kaduna's, about two weeks of collections even for
Taraba. The expensive items are not purchasable: operational autonomy for
the revenue service, and the fight with people who live on cash collection.

**Cost does not explain why poor states have not done this.**
**[Certified — Lagos Eko Revenue Plus planning documents; Kwara, Abia,
Enugu implementations]** · Chapter 1, `Blueprint/PILOT_BRIEF.md`
