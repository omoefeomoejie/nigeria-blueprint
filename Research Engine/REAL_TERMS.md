# Real-Terms Restatement

*Red team, Tier 3.2: most of our headline revenue figures are nominal naira,
in a period when the currency collapsed. This restates them, and one of our
cases does not survive.*

---

## The deflator

| Year | Average CPI inflation |
|---|---|
| 2023 | ~25% |
| 2024 | **31.4%** |
| 2025 | **23.01%** |

[Certified — FocusEconomics, Statista/IMF, NBS via Intelpoint]

Cumulatively: **₦1 in early 2023 ≈ ₦0.62 by end-2024 ≈ ₦0.50 by end-2025.**
Prices roughly doubled across exactly the window in which every revenue
success this project cites occurred.

**Methodological caveat, stated plainly.** NBS rebased the CPI from 2009 to
2024 during this period and later changed the reference from December 2024
to a 12-month 2024 average. Under the old methodology, December 2025
inflation would have printed at 31.2% rather than 15.15%. So these
deflations are indicative, not precise. A proper restatement would use the
GDP deflator on a consistent series. **What follows is directionally sound
and should not be quoted to two decimal places.**

## The restatement

### Enugu — survives, at roughly half the drama
- **Nominal:** ₦37.6bn (2023) → ₦180.5bn (2024) → ₦406.8bn (2025).
  Reported by us as 4.8× then +125%.
- **Real (2023 naira, approximate):** ₦37.6bn → ~₦112bn → ~₦200bn.
- **Real growth: about 5.3× over two years**, against the ~10.8× the nominal
  figures imply.

Still extraordinary. Still the strongest revenue case in Nigeria. But we
have been quoting a number that is roughly double the real one.

### Osun — **does not survive as a growth claim**
- **Nominal:** ₦22.4bn (2021) → ₦58.8bn (2025). Reported by us as +162%.
- **Real:** approximately **flat to modestly positive**. Most of the nominal
  gain is price level, not revenue.

**Consequence:** Osun's *mechanism* (tax harmonisation plus digitisation,
independently confirmed by Dataphyte) remains Certified. Its *growth* should
be **withdrawn from the Blueprint's evidence base**. We were using it as one
of three layer-1 replications; it survives as a mechanism example and fails
as a magnitude example.

This is the first case in the project retired by our own arithmetic rather
than by contrary evidence.

### Kwara — survives, reduced
- **Nominal:** +262.8% (2020–24).
- **Real:** approximately **+40–50%**.

Real growth, genuine case, and the succession-survival finding (statutory
authority through a hostile 2019 handover) is unaffected because it is not a
magnitude claim. But it is not the headline it appeared to be.

### Lagos — unaffected
Already Confounded on geography. Deflation changes nothing about a case we
had already demoted.

### Oil derivation — **strengthens**
- **Nominal:** ₦671.9bn (2024) → ₦1.51tn (2025).
- **Real:** still a large increase after 23% inflation.

So the oil finding gets sharper, not weaker: **the most money in real terms
sits with the worst delivery record in the federation.**

### The ₦332bn idle education money — unaffected
It is a stock sitting still, not a growth rate. Its scandal is that it is
not moving at all.

   for the *layers*; the accompanying growth figures are corrected.
4. **Sector 09:** finding strengthened.

## The lesson for the engine

**Every nominal figure in a high-inflation economy is a claim about the
currency as much as about the thing being measured.** We ran eight nodes and
eight sector briefs before applying a deflator, and it took an adversarial
pass over our own work to notice.

Rule candidate, not yet earned: *deflate before you celebrate.* It waits for
a second case, per Law Zero — though the honest reading is that this one case
cost us a replication, which is expensive enough to argue it has been paid
for.
