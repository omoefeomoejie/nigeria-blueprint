# Node 5 — Decision

**Node:** Constitutional
**Question investigated:** Which constitutional provisions causally produce
the dysfunctions Nodes 1–4 documented, and has any comparable federation
reformed such provisions without rupture?
**Status:** Decision-grade.

---

## The decision

**Nigeria's constitution is reformable without rupture — but success is
route-matched to the loser's loss-type. The Blueprint's constitutional
strategy is sequenced, not monolithic: take every burden-shedding devolution
now; build decade-scale ratification constituencies for the money reforms;
treat the s.162(2) mandatory-transmission amendment (the Indian
Finance-Commission transplant) as the keystone — honestly noting the
gate-holder must sign the key.**

**Confidence: ~65%.** The route-matching pattern rests on three clean paired
cases within four years. Priced down because n=3, the keystone amendment's
own passage is untested, and constituency-building timescales (~decade) are
inferred from one trajectory (LG ratification 2015→2023).

---

## Evidence ledger

**Certified (independent/multi-source):**
- The indicted provisions, assembled by Nodes 1–4: FAAC formula (s.162,
  frozen since 1992: 52.68/26.72/20.60), federal character (encodes group
  allocation), ss. 214–215 (police monopoly), s.162 joint accounts.
- The gate: 2022 RMAFC proposal (45.17/29.79/21.04, nationally consulted)
  died untransmitted on the President's desk; a predecessor did the same;
  RMAFC formally pleads for a s.162(2) transmission deadline.
- India (structural match): devolution 32%→42% (14th Finance Commission),
  GST Council (Art 279A, states hold 2/3 votes), CSS 75:25→50:50 — repeated
  fiscal-settlement reform without rupture, via a constitutionalized
  *renegotiation process* (Art 280 five-yearly commission; Art 281 mandatory
  tabling), not constitutionalized percentages.
- Electricity devolution: constitutional amendment (Mar 2023) + Electricity
  Act (Jun 2023); state electricity markets live; Aba model operating.
- LG autonomy: ratification failed 2015 (0 states) / 2019 / 2023 (short by
  six, trend rising); Supreme Court win July 2024; implementation defied —
  joint accounts persist, ~₦10tn state-controlled.

**Contested:**
- India-transplant fit: India's convention-based acceptance has no Nigerian
  equivalent; Rule 5 precondition gap acknowledged.

**Owed:**
- Whether any president will sign a s.162(2) amendment curbing his own gate
  (possible windows: final-term presidents; reform-coalition NASS).
- The current (2025–26) RMAFC review's fate — live test of the whole finding.

## Recommendation
1. Constitutional chapter is a **sequenced route-map**, not a rewrite demand:
   (a) burden-shedding devolutions now (each grows unit capacity —
   units-first fuel); (b) money reforms via decade-scale ratification
   constituencies (the LG trajectory is the template); (c) keystone: s.162(2)
   transmission deadline + Finance-Commission-style periodic process.
2. Judicial route: use for declarations, never rely on for disbursements;
   pair every ruling with the enabling amendment and an organized
   beneficiary constituency (Rules 6/7/9).
3. The three prior nodes' indicted provisions are confirmed as the
   constitutional chapter's defendants; Node 3's "walls" line corrected
   (Mistakes 5.1).

## Live-test update (Engine v0.15 — 15 Aug 2026)

**Confidence ~65% → ~70%.** The 2025–26 RMAFC review completed after
nationwide consultation; the harmonised report and legislative proposals are
described as **"ready for transmission to the appropriate authorities."**
Not transmitted — the fifth occurrence of the same gate state since 2001.

**Within-institution paired case (strongest Rule 9 confirmation to date):**
in the same briefing cycle, the *remuneration* stream moved — judicial
office-holders' pay became an Act (2025); the executive/legislative pay bill
is "expected to be transmitted"; and RMAFC sent the political salaries
review directly to the President in July 2026. Same commission, same gate,
same year: the reform that pays officeholders travels; the reform that would
cut the federal share (52.68% → ~45%) waits. Velocity sorted by whether the
gate-holder gains or loses.

**Behavioural signature:** RMAFC is optimising around its blocked lever —
National Revenue Dashboard, FAAN/NASRDA satellite-geospatial revenue
identification, tighter 13% derivation monitoring. An institution unable to
change the formula is maximising collection instead.

**Watch item (not a research gap):** transmission may still occur. If it
does, this finding weakens and must be revised by commit. Only time resolves
it.

## Correction (Engine v0.17 — from Sector Brief 02, Power)

Recommendation 1(a) of this decision ("take every burden-shedding devolution
now") is **amended**: take it *and build the absorptive capacity first*.
The 2023 electricity devolution — 16 states by early 2026, NERC-published —
confirmed the route-matching finding (burden-shedding doors open) and
simultaneously showed that **an acquired power without regulatory capacity
degrades governance**: non-functional regulators, licences outside the legal
framework, no measurable supply improvement two years in. See Mistakes 8.1.
Confidence unchanged (~70%) — the gate finding is strengthened; only the
prescription is corrected.
