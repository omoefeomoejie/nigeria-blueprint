# Skill: Comparative Statecraft Engine — v0.1

**What this skill does**

Given a national problem, it finds where the problem has been solved, extracts
the *transferable* part of the solution, and rejects the parts that only worked
because of local accidents. It is the engine's defence against the most common
reform error: copying what a success *did* instead of what actually *caused* the
success.

**Version note.** This is v0.1. It contains only rules that Node 1 paid for.
It is deliberately small. It will grow one case at a time. Empty sections below
are honest placeholders, not omissions — they mark where future nodes will
teach rules this skill does not yet have the right to assert.

## Rules earned so far (each with a birth certificate)

### Rule 1 — Beware confounded success stories
Before treating a success as a model, separate the *reform* from the *endowment*.
A case is confounded when its outcome could be explained by advantages no one
can copy.

> **Origin — Node 1:** Lagos funds ~72% of itself (only 28.1% from FAAC) and
> runs a genuinely advanced digital tax authority (LIRS E-Tax, 800k+ properties
> enumerated). But Lagos also holds ~a third of national GDP, ~70% of foreign
> trade inflow, and the country's corporate headquarters. Its IGR success cannot
> be separated from that endowment, so Lagos proves *less* than it appears.
> Telling Taraba (IGR ₦10.87bn) to "be like Lagos" is telling it to first acquire
> a seaport. Confounded → demoted from proof to illustration.

### Rule 2 — Not all IGR growth is the same phenomenon
"Revenue went up" is not a finding until it is classified. At least three
distinct things hide under one number, and only one is the transferable engine:
- **Automation / compliance growth** — systematised digital collection, plugged
  leakages, widened net (incl. informal sector). *This is the replicable
  mechanism.*
- **Extractive / windfall growth** — oil- or resource-linked corporate tax. Rents
  wearing a tax costume. Not transferable; not even really IGR in spirit.
- **Optics growth** — the number rises but capital execution stays poor. A vanity
  metric; the state collects more and still cannot *do* more.

> **Origin — Node 1:** The Businessfront analysis separated Enugu/Kaduna
> (automation, compliance, informal-net widening) from Rivers/Delta (oil-linked
> corporate tax) from Ekiti/Taraba (revenue up, capital execution poor). The
> three-way split was invisible in the raw NBS totals and changed what counts
> as proof.

### Rule 2.1 — Decompose revenue by source before classifying the mechanism
"IGR increased" is insufficient evidence of anything. Before judging whether a
revenue reform is transferable, break the number into its sources — tax vs.
recurring non-tax (licences, dividends, commercial income) vs. one-off non-tax
(asset sales, recoveries) vs. royalties vs. administrative reform. These are
fundamentally different mechanisms with different transferability, and they hide
inside one figure.

This rule deliberately does **not** prejudge any source as good or bad.
Non-tax revenue is not inherently suspect — it is *undifferentiated*, and
must be decomposed, not dismissed. The rule forces decomposition before
interpretation; it does not pre-load the interpretation.

> **Origin — Node 1 (Enugu):** We had filed Enugu under "automation broadened the
> tax base." Decomposition broke that: of ₦180.5bn (2024), only ₦30bn was tax —
> ₦150bn was non-tax (asset recovery / dormant-asset revitalisation); by 2025,
> non-tax was 87.4% of ₦406.7bn. The aggregate "IGR up 4.8×" concealed a
> revenue-*source* story entirely different from the tax-broadening one we
> assumed. (The earlier instinct to instead call non-tax growth "suspect like
> rents" was itself rejected in review — that prejudged what decomposition is
> supposed to examine. See Mistakes 1.5.)

### Rule 3 — Prefer independent audits over interested claims
Weight a source by its stake in the answer. A governor's press office, a
gubernatorial aide, or an election-season op-ed describing *why* a number moved
is advocacy, not audit. Raw bureau statistics and independent civic audits
(e.g. BudgIT, NBS, World Bank fiscal-transparency rankings) outrank them.

> **Origin — Node 1:** Osun's IGR rise (₦27.7bn → ₦54.7bn) is real in the NBS
> numbers, but every available *explanation* of the mechanism traced to a
> Special Assistant to the Governor or a 2026-election-framed op-ed. The growth
> is logged as **contested, not certified**, pending an independent source —
> precisely because the interested sources cannot close the question.

### Rule 4 — Separate the revenue SOURCE from the institutional layers
The transferable part of a fiscal success is usually **not** how the money
enters government (that is often endowment-bound: Lagos's geography, Enugu's
recoverable assets). The transferable part is three institutional layers that
sit underneath, and that copy regardless of source:

1. **Collection integrity** — plug leakages; traceability; digitisation;
   eliminate cash handling.
2. **Fiscal management** — budgeting, allocation, expenditure discipline.
3. **Execution / conversion** — turn collections into delivered projects, so
   revenue is not merely accumulated (defeats the "optics" failure of Rule 2).

When evaluating a case for transfer: discard the source if it is an endowment,
and ask whether these three layers improved. They are the portable mechanism.

> **Origin — Node 1:** Lagos and Enugu have *opposite* revenue sources (commercial
> geography vs. asset recovery) — both confounded, neither copyable. But both
> show the same three layers: Enugu killed cash collection across all MDAs
> (integrity), reversed a recurrent-heavy budget to ~79–86% capex (management),
> and converted it into built infrastructure (execution), independently
> corroborated by BudgIT. The layers, not the source, are what a structurally
> ordinary state could actually adopt.

## The procedure (apply Rules 1 → 2 → 2.1 → 3 → 4)

1. **Find structural matches, not aspirational ones.** Prefer states/nations that
   started where the target started (poor, diverse, low-capacity) over admired
   exemplars that were already strong before they reformed.
2. **Run the confounding check (Rule 1).** What endowment, not reform, could
   explain this? If the reform can't be isolated, demote the case.
3. **Decompose the revenue by source (Rule 2.1).** Break "IGR up" into tax /
   recurring non-tax / one-off non-tax / royalties / administrative reform. Do
   not interpret before decomposing. Do not prejudge any source.
4. **Classify the gain (Rule 2).** Given the decomposition, which phenomena are
   present? Flag endowment-bound sources as confounded.
5. **Separate source from layers (Rule 4).** Discard endowment-bound sources;
   ask whether collection integrity, fiscal management, and execution improved.
   Those are the transferable mechanism.
6. **Weight the sources (Rule 3).** Is the *mechanism story* told by an audit or
   by someone with a stake? Certified vs. contested.
7. **State what is owed.** Name the single source or fact that would move the case
   up a status.
