# Foundations — The Strategy Layer (Pre-Evidence)

This file preserves the project's founding reasoning, worked out before the
Research Engine existed. **Its epistemic status is: candidate doctrine.**
Under the Evidence Standard, nothing below is Certified; each load-bearing
claim is listed at the end with the node expected to test it. This document
is allowed to be wrong — that is why the engine exists.

---

## 1. What the whole project is

**"How To Make Nigeria Great"** — a publicly published blueprint for Nigeria's
transformation within a generation, connected to the *Hand of Nigeria* podcast
and a path toward influence in Nigerian public life.

**The bet:** credibility through verifiability. Where politicians offer
promises ("I'll fix power") with nothing behind them, this project publishes
the plan itself — problem → mechanism → sequence → metrics → kill conditions —
so any skeptic can open it to page 40 and check. The blueprint does not need to
be read by everyone; it needs to be *checkable* by anyone. Its existence is
what makes the doctrine serious.

**Two artifacts, never confused:**
- **The Blueprint** (this folder, eventually): what the public reads.
- **The Research Engine** (`../Research Engine/`): the machine that certifies
  the Blueprint's claims are true. Readers see the first; the second is why
  they can trust it.

## 2. The build order

**Doctrine → Blueprint → Movement.** Each layer is the proof of seriousness
for the next: the doctrine earns attention, the blueprint earns belief, the
movement earns power. Most movements start at Movement and die of having
nothing underneath but a personality.

## 3. The diagnosis (candidate)

Four-layer descent:
1. **Visible problems** — corruption, insecurity, electricity, poverty,
   unemployment, education, healthcare.
2. **Structural causes** — weak institutions, oil dependence, elite capture,
   low trust, regulatory failure.
3. **Root causes** — weak accountability, poor leadership selection,
   misaligned incentives, low state capacity.
4. **First principle:** *the Nigerian state lacks the capacity and incentives
   to consistently act in the long-term interests of its citizens.*

**Root disease (candidate): misaligned incentives within a low-capacity
state.** Corruption is a symptom. Tribe and religion are not causes but
*adaptations* to state failure — the survival systems people fall back on when
courts, police, and government cannot be trusted. Oil is fuel, not engine: the
rentier mechanism (a state funded by rents does not need productive citizens,
so it has no structural reason to serve them) explains why the feedback loop
between government performance and citizen welfare is broken — but countries
with identical resources diverged (Norway, Botswana vs. others), so the engine
is the incentive structure, not the resource.

**Primary lever: state capacity. Supporting lever: accountability.** They form
a loop: enter through capacity for visible early wins, but hardwire
accountability into everything built, so the machine does not become a more
efficient extraction tool for the next bad actor.

## 4. The highest design standard

**Not achieved — irreversible.** Reformers leave accomplishments; founders
leave machines that survive their worst successor. The design question for
every chapter: *"Would this still work under a mediocre or hostile successor?"*
— a state "impossible to run badly."

*Post-evidence refinement (Node 2, Rule 7):* irreversibility splits into
**stock** (one-time changes that persist by default) and **flow** (ongoing
practices that die at succession unless housed in a statutory institution with
an organized constituency). Every "irreversible" claim must now be made
separately for each. Kaduna is the named case: dismissals persisted, testing
died with the founder.

## 5. The five irreversibles (candidate targets, 10-year horizon)

1. **A productive tax state** — government funded by economic activity, not
   rents; changes the citizen–state bargain by incentive, not appeal.
   *(Touched by Node 1: the transferable object is the three institutional
   layers — collection integrity, fiscal management, execution — not any
   revenue source.)*
2. **A meritocratic leadership pipeline** — the system manufactures
   competence rather than hoping for it. *(Touched by Node 2: executable at
   state level; must be designed against succession, not just resistance;
   federal character is the constitutional counter-force.)*
3. **A high-capacity civil service** — politicians come and go; execution
   lives in the service.
4. **A literacy and skills revolution** — the test: can a Nigerian child born
   today compete with a child born anywhere?
5. **A self-reinforcing accountability system** — corruption made increasingly
   expensive and difficult: traceable transactions, institutions auditing each
   other, citizens able to verify spending.

Deliberately absent: electricity, roads, healthcare, housing — outputs of the
five machines, not chapters that start the book.

## 6. The reference doctrine

**Judge mechanisms, not flags.** China, Singapore, Rwanda, South Korea are not
models to copy; they are existence proofs that a poor, written-off nation can
rise in one generation — and evidence for non-negotiables shared across
otherwise opposite systems (state capacity to execute; corruption fought at
survival priority; long horizons; people as the asset; early visible wins).
If a policy-mechanism is proven to work for humanity's benefit, its country of
origin is not a reason to reason against it. Every borrowed mechanism still
owes the transfer test — preconditions, Nigerian constraints, adaptation
(now formalized as Comparative Statecraft Engine Rules 5–7).

## 7. The chapter framework (11 fields)

Every Blueprint chapter runs the same skeleton: 1 Desired Outcome (measurable),
2 Structural Matches (not aspirational), 3 Mechanism (not policy),
4 Preconditions, 5 Nigerian Constraints, 6 Nigerian Adaptation, 7 Political
Economy (who loses, how they fight — and per Node 2, how they *wait*),
8 Early-Win Pilot, 9 Scale Strategy, 10 Success Metrics, 11 Kill Condition.
Above all eleven: the irreversibility test (stock and flow separately).

## 8. The audience doctrine

Written for the 90% — the motor-park reader, primary-school accessible, street
grit, Nigerianisms, short chapters, conversational not lecturing. **No insults
to leaders past or present**; if leadership must be addressed: Commend,
Criticize, Commend. This is a solution book, not a trauma book — medicine, not
blame ("leave James first; heal yourself"). Agency over anger: when enough
people hold the same clear picture of what right looks like, victim-blaming
dissolves and a critical mass (even ~10%) becomes unstoppable pressure.
Distribution goal: ubiquity — markets, motor parks; cost never a barrier;
concepts sticky enough to spread by conversation.

## 9. The unresolved fork (logged, not settled)

**Centre vs. units** (= Research Engine Assumptions.md #1): does Nigeria change
by capturing the centre and reforming downward, or by willing states proving a
better model until the centre is forced to follow? Nearly all tactics so far
quietly assume units-first; the "Constitution on trial" framing assumes
centre-first. Both nodes have touched it; neither resolved it. No chapter may
silently assume an answer.

**The constitutional convergence (evidence-fed):** two independent nodes have
now arrived at the Constitution as a core system node — Node 1 (revenue
allocation formula) and Node 2 (federal character principle). The constitutional
question is the one place the pilot-and-scale method cannot run: it cannot be
piloted in a willing state, and its jury is composed of its beneficiaries.
