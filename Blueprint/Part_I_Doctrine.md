# Part I — What We Believe

*Nine lines. Each one short enough to carry, and each one backed by an
investigation you can open and attack. This chapter explains them. The lines
themselves are the part that travels.*

---

## Before the lines: why this book is different

Every Nigerian has heard a plan. Power will be fixed. Roads will come. The
economy will be diversified. What has never been offered is the *working*:
the evidence, the mechanism, the cost of being wrong.

So this book makes one unusual promise, and it is the ninth line: **don't
believe us — check it.** Everything here is published — the claims, the
sources, the confidence levels, and, unusually, the record of everywhere we
changed our minds. We were wrong about Lagos. We were wrong about what drove
Enugu. We were wrong about Vietnam. Those reversals are written down, dated,
and public, because a movement that cannot change its mind in daylight
becomes an ideology, and ideology cannot govern reality.

---

## 1. The Feeding Rule
> *A government that eats from your work must answer your voice. A government
> that eats oil answers nobody.*

Thirty of thirty-six states get 71–92% of their money from oil shared in
Abuja [Certified, Node 1]. A government funded without you does not need you
to be productive, safe, or educated. It is not that our leaders are uniquely
wicked; it is that nothing in the structure makes serving you the way to
keep power. Change what the state eats, and you change what it notices.
→ *Chapter 1*

## 2. The Three Jobs
> *Block the leak. Plan the money. Show the work.*

Two Nigerian states with completely opposite sources of money — Lagos with
its ports, Enugu with recovered assets — turned out to share the same three
institutional layers underneath [Certified, Node 1]. Collection integrity.
Fiscal management. Conversion into delivered projects. The source is luck;
the three jobs are the copyable part.
→ *Chapters 1, 4*

## 3. The Builder's Rule
> *Don't build on one good man. Build what a bad man cannot scatter.*

In one state, in one handover, two reforms met opposite fates: the teacher
competency testing stopped when its governor left; the open-government
machinery survived and expanded [Certified, Nodes 2 and 4]. The difference
was not quality — it was housing. What sits in law with a constituency
lives; what sits in one man's will dies with his tenure.
→ *Every chapter's irreversibility test*

## 4. The Seat Rule
> *Test the person before the seat — and let the law hold the test, not the oga.*

Merit selection has been executed in Nigeria at scale, against strikes and
lawsuits [Certified, Node 2]. But its instrument was contestable and its
testing cycle unhoused — so it was outwaited. Publish the test before anyone
sits; put a statutory board in charge; and be honest that no Nigerian case
has yet proved the link to better service [Owed, Node 2].
→ *Chapter 2*

## 5. The Trace Rule
> *Money you can trace is money that can reach you.*

A Nigerian state holds a perfect independent transparency score [Certified,
Node 4]. Internationally, transparent bidding measurably increases
competition and cuts corruption risk. But publishing is only the first of
four links — usability, binding participation, and measured savings are
where Nigeria has built nothing yet. That is a job, not a slogan.
→ *Chapter 4*

## 6. The Ground and the Badge
> *Safety comes when those who know the ground join those who hold the power
> — under law.*

Community members drove an insurgency out of a city that federal force alone
could not hold, because they could tell neighbour from infiltrator
[Certified, Node 3]. The same literature records their abuses. So: local
knowledge, lawful force, published complaints — all three, or none.
→ *Chapter 3*

## 7. The Doors Rule
> *Every wall has a door somewhere. Take the open doors first.*

The revenue formula has not moved since 1992 because reform must pass the
desk of the tier that loses [Certified, Node 5]. Yet the Constitution was
amended peacefully in 2023 to let states generate power — because that door
cost the centre a burden, not money. Read which gate is held by whom, take
what is open now, and build coalitions for the rest.
→ *Chapter 5*

## 8. The Medicine Rule
> *Blame builds nothing. We did not come to fight anybody; we came to build.*

Tribe and religion are not Nigeria's disease. Ethnic identification here
swings twenty points in eighteen months, rises at election time, and hardens
when the state treats people unfairly [Certified, Node 6]. People shelter in
tribe when the state fails them, and politicians reach for it when they are
competing over one central pot. Fix the state, shrink the pot, and the
shelter is needed less. Fight your neighbour and nothing is fixed at all.
→ *Chapters 5, 6*

## 9. The Signature
> *Don't believe us — check it. Everything is written, so anybody can test it.*

Nigeria's own fifty-year-old service scheme measurably reduces ethnic bias
in the people it posts across the country — proven with the strongest
evidence in this book [Certified, Node 6]. We did not invent it. We found
it, checked it, and wrote down what would prove us wrong.
→ *The Research Engine, and every chapter's kill condition*

## The Wager

> *When enough of us know the same truth and move, the country moves.*

**Status: Untested** — our bet, not our finding [Part V]. We name it as a
wager because everything else in this book is required to name its evidence,
and this belief cannot yet name any.

---

## What the doctrine asks of you

Carry one line. Check one number about your own state. Join one thing that
mixes you with people unlike you — the one instruction here with causal
proof behind it. And if you find us wrong, say so publicly: we keep a file
of our own errors, and yours will be added with your name on it.
