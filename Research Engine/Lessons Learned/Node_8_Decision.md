# Node 8 — Decision

**Node:** Elections
**Question investigated:** Has any measurable improvement in Nigerian
election integrity been produced by a specific mechanism — and did it
survive the next cycle?
**Status:** Decision-grade.

---

## The decision

**Election-integrity technology in Nigeria worked exactly as far as its
legal housing allowed, and no further. BVAS accredited voters successfully;
IReV published results with no legal force, and the courts said so
explicitly. The 2026 Act has now housed transmission in statute — but the
clause that carried the mechanism (real-time upload while observers are
still present) was voted down, restored under protest, and the final text is
read differently by credible sources. The Blueprint's position: statutory
housing is necessary and was achieved; the binding question is timing, and
it is unresolved until 2027.**

**Confidence: ~70%.** High on the mechanism-and-housing finding (court
rulings are unambiguous). Priced down because credible sources conflict on
what the signed 2026 text actually requires, and because the 2027 election
is the only real test.

---

## Evidence ledger

**Certified:**
- **What worked:** BVAS accreditation performed; INEC officials testified
  that voting ran smoothly and that Senate/House results transmitted freely
  before a device error on the presidential result.
- **What failed:** only **31.8% of polling unit results** were uploaded to
  IReV within 36 hours of polls closing; collation centres in Lagos,
  Rivers, Kogi and several northern states announced figures differing
  materially from BVAS records at the units.
- **The courts closed the door:** the Presidential Election Petitions Court
  held the Electoral Act 2022 made **no provision for electronic
  transmission**, and only BVAS was mandatory. The Supreme Court held that
  a petitioner **cannot rely on IReV or INEC's server** to prove
  over-voting; only BVAS, the voter register, or Form EC8A are admissible.
  IReV was "merely a public viewing portal… no legal force."
- **The evidentiary trap:** INEC reconfigured BVAS machines in the three
  weeks between the February and March 2023 elections, so the machines had
  no real evidential value at hearing — yet courts still required them
  physically produced, with conflicting lower-court rulings (PLAC).
- **The legislative sequence:** 4 Feb 2026 Senate rejects mandatory
  real-time transmission → nationwide protests → 10 Feb emergency session
  passes a revised Clause 60(3) → 18 Feb presidential assent, ~24 hours
  after harmonisation.

**Contested — recorded unresolved:**
- **What the 2026 Act requires.** Several analyses state §60(3) gives IReV
  statutory recognition and mandates electronic transmission of Form EC8A,
  "an obligation that did not exist under the 2022 Act." Another states the
  signed version leaves transmission optional, retains manual Form EC8A as
  the primary legal record, and leaves timing to INEC discretion, with
  opposition members walking out. **Most probable reconciliation:**
  transmission became mandatory; **real-time** transmission did not.
- **Incapacity vs subversion in 2023.** BudgIT emphasises connectivity,
  absent backend expertise and logistics; scholarly analysis argues the
  failure was "less because of the equipment itself, and more because of
  deliberate subversion by those entrusted to manage the process."
  Unadjudicated [Rule 8].
- **The feasibility defence.** The President cited broadband coverage,
  technical capacity and hacking risk; ALTON countered with NCC data
  showing **over 70% nationwide 3G/4G coverage**.

**Owed / watch:**
- The 2027 elections (presidential 16 Jan; governorship 6 Feb) — the only
  test that settles this.
- The operative text of §60(3) as gazetted, read directly.

## What this node adds to the engine

**Rule 7 vindicated in its most consequential domain.** IReV was a *flow*
built administratively — no statute, no legal duty, therefore no
consequence when it failed. The courts did not rule that INEC behaved well;
they ruled that INEC had no binding obligation to breach. An unhoused
transparency mechanism is a courtesy, and courtesies fail under pressure.

**Rule 9 confirmed at speed.** The gate opened in ~24 hours for the version
the gate-holders could accept, and closed on the clause that would have
bound them. Compare Node 5: the revenue-formula proposal, which costs the
centre money, has sat untransmitted since 2022. Same legislature, same
presidency, opposite velocities — sorted by loss-type.

**New observation (not yet a rule):** the mechanism's entire force lived in
one timing word. Upload *while agents and citizens are still present* closes
the manipulation window between polling unit and collation centre; upload
"in a manner prescribed" reopens it. **Reforms can be defeated by amending
their timing rather than their existence** — a subtler defeat than
repeal, and harder to protest. Logged; a second case would earn it.

---

## Recommendation
1. The elections chapter's central ask is **timing, not technology**:
   statutory real-time upload at the polling unit, with observers present.
   The technology already works; the window is the vulnerability.
2. Protect the evidence chain: prohibit reconfiguration of accreditation
   devices before petitions are heard, and make server data admissible.
3. Treat the 2026 Act as **housing achieved, bindingness unresolved** —
   and say so publicly rather than celebrating or dismissing it.
4. State electoral commissions (local government polls) are unexamined and
   are the weakest link in the system. **Owed.**

---

**Assumption touched:** units-first. Elections are the one domain where the
units-first logic weakens — state electoral commissions are widely regarded
as the least credible tier, so devolving election administration further
would likely worsen integrity. Recorded as a boundary condition on
Assumption 1.
