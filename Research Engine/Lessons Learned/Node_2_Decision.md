# Node 2 — Decision

**Node:** Leadership Selection
**Question investigated:** Has any Nigerian state (or structurally comparable
country) measurably changed the competence of its public administrators through
a selection/recruitment reform — and did service delivery measurably follow?
**Status:** Decision-grade (uncertainties recorded and priced).

---

## The decision

**Merit-based selection reform is executable in Nigeria at state level — but
execution is the easy half. The binding constraints are (a) preconditions,
(b) the organized loser's patience, and (c) institutional housing for the flow.
The Blueprint's meritocratic-pipeline chapter must be designed against
succession, not against resistance.**

**Confidence: ~60%.** High that the mechanism is real (Georgia, independently
certified) and executable in Nigeria (Kaduna, executed and sustained for five
years against organized resistance). Priced down because the Nigerian case's
*outcome* leg (did children learn more?) is unfilled and possibly unmeasured,
and because the flow failed the succession test — meaning no case yet shows a
Nigerian selection reform that is both executed *and* irreversible.

---

## Evidence ledger

**Certified (independent):**
- Georgia 2004: ~16,000 traffic police dismissed at once; rebuilt force;
  petty corruption virtually eliminated (World Bank, OECD); police third most
  trusted institution by 2009; survived succession via institutional housing.
  Caveats: political interference persisted; corruption changed shape;
  stalled post-2017; World Bank declines growth-causality claims.
- Kaduna 2017–2022: ~21,780 dismissed on a primary-four benchmark; ~25,000
  recruited through screening; repeated (2021) and institutionalized as
  continuous assessment (2022); survived NLC strike, court challenge, and the
  NUT president's dismissal. Multi-outlet corroboration.
- Kaduna post-2023: no test since 2021; successor pivoted to
  welfare-and-retention (retirement 60→65, rural allowances, ₦548m NUT
  release); dismissals never reversed; recruits remain.

**Contested:**
- Kaduna's measurement instrument: independent scoresheet audit found
  computation errors and a pass-mark (75%) far above the West African credit
  standard (50%); sacked-teachers' forum alleges payroll-reduction motive.

**Rejected:**
- "2021 vs 2017 failure rates show the new workforce is dramatically better."
  (Bar moved 75% → 40%. See Mistakes 2.1.)
- "Surviving resistance under the founder demonstrates irreversibility."
  (See Mistakes 2.2/2.3.)

**Owed:**
- Kaduna pupil learning outcomes attributable to the replacement (may be
  unmeasured — evaluators report limited impact evidence).
- A Nigerian selection reform that survived succession (does one exist?).

**Constitutional finding (feeds the constitutional node):**
- The federal character principle constitutionally encodes group-allocation
  over merit in public appointments — Node 2 independently converges with
  Node 1 (revenue formula) on the Constitution as a core system node.

---

## Recommendation
1. The Blueprint's meritocratic-pipeline chapter is built on **selection +
   preconditions + succession design**, not selection alone: every proposed
   reform names its mandate source, its funding for pay reform, its organized
   loser and their waiting game, and the statutory institution + constituency
   that owns the flow after the founder exits.
2. Pilot template (18-month, willing state): competency-based recruitment into
   ONE service corridor (e.g. teaching or revenue service) with (a) published
   instrument and thresholds fixed in advance (answers the Contested leg),
   (b) an outcome metric named on day one (answers the Owed leg),
   (c) a statutory board, not a governor's practice, as owner (answers Rule 7).
3. Proceed to Node 3.
