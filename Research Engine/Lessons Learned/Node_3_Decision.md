# Node 3 — Decision

**Node:** Security
**Question investigated:** Under Nigeria's federal police monopoly, has any
state or sub-national actor measurably and durably reduced violent insecurity
through state-level action — and by what mechanism?
**Status:** Decision-grade (uncertainties recorded and priced).

---

## The decision

**Sub-national security action can measurably matter — but the certified
mechanism is community-intelligence complementarity under a constitutional
ceiling, not policing substitution. The Blueprint's security chapter must be
built on that mechanism, designed against its documented abuse and
militia-drift risks, and honest that broad state security outfits are unproven
and may be unmeasurable.**

**Confidence: ~65%.** High on the CJTF mechanism (independently corroborated,
with a within-case counterfactual). Priced down because outfit-level outcome
attribution failed everywhere it was attempted (Amotekun), the certified
mechanism arose under an existential-threat precondition that may not
generalize, and abuse/drift risks are documented, not hypothetical.

---

## Evidence ledger

**Certified (independent):**
- CJTF (Borno, 2013–): expelled Boko Haram from Maiduguri at 100+ member
  deaths; mechanism — local identification/language/terrain intelligence
  supplied to federal forces, the exact capability those forces lacked
  (CFR, CIVIC, peer-reviewed corroboration).
- Within-case contrast: Maiduguri held; comparable LGAs without a strong
  community organ were attacked and controlled.
- Institutional route-around: Borno funds CJTF ₦352m/yr via its Ministry of
  Justice — a state financing security despite ss. 214–215.
- Constitutional ceiling: state police prohibited (ss. 214–215, 1999
  Constitution). Third consecutive node converging on the Constitution.

**Contested:**
- "Amotekun reduced crime": perception significantly improved (Ondo study)
  while Southwest fatalities ~doubled 2020–24, above the national trend;
  base effects + southward threat migration make raw attribution impossible
  in either direction. Possibly unmeasurable with public data.
- Kaduna "zero violent conflict since 2023": interested source only.

**Documented risks (not disputed):**
- CJTF abuses against civilians ("white devil" literature), de facto
  authority drift, demobilization problem.

**Owed:**
- A comparative Amotekun-state vs non-Amotekun-Southern-state event-level
  analysis (difference-in-differences shape), if data permits.
- Any independent audit of the Kaduna Peace Model claim.

---

## Recommendation
1. Security chapter mechanism: **statutory community-intelligence organs**
   feeding federal/joint forces — built-in abuse controls (vetting, legal
   accountability, sunset/absorption path) designed from Rule 6/7 lessons:
   statutory housing, named constituency, succession plan.
2. Do not build the chapter on the state-security-outfit model (Amotekun
   class) until attribution is possible; an unmeasurable mechanism cannot
   anchor a checkable Blueprint.
3. The constitutional ceiling is now load-bearing for three nodes; the
   constitutional chapter inherits ss. 214–215 alongside the revenue formula
   and federal character.
4. Proceed to Node 4.
