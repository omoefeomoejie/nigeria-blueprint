# How To Join

*There is an exam. Here is why, and here is exactly how it works.*

---

## Why an exam at all

Three reasons.

**A movement whose members cannot explain it becomes a crowd.** A crowd
repeats things it cannot defend, loses the first serious argument it meets,
and can be bought, split, or turned by whoever pays last. You have watched
this happen to every Nigerian movement of your lifetime.

**We are asking Nigeria to be run by people who can do the job.** A movement
that demands merit in the civil service while admitting anybody who turns up
has already lost that argument, and deserves to.

**And it changes what a member is.** Everywhere else, a member is somebody
who registered. Here, a member is somebody who has proved they understand the
programme.

**That is the one thing money cannot buy quickly.**

---

## It is not the Chinese model, and the reason matters

The obvious comparison is the Chinese Communist Party, which also examines
its members. We looked at it and rejected it — using our own evidence.

The CCP sets its own exam, marks its own exam, promotes its own passers, and
audits itself.

That is the purest example anywhere of an organisation keeping its own score.

And our whole book says what that produces. It is the same structure that
gave Lagos 375 seats out of 376, and Osun 332 out of 332. **Where the body
being measured controls the measurement, you get a monopoly, not merit.**

So a movement that sets and marks its own membership test with nobody outside
checking would have built, in miniature, the exact thing its own book calls
broken.

**We keep the exam. We reject the model.**

The governance comes from professional bodies instead. ICAN — the accountants
— publishes its syllabus, publishes a 50% pass mark, and examines in more
than thirty Nigerian cities. That is the standard we copy.

---

## The three levels

**Level 1 — What we found.** The findings themselves. What is broken, what
works, what it costs. *40 questions. Pass mark 50%.*

**Level 2 — How we know.** Why we believe what we believe. Why we demoted
Lagos. Why we withdrew Osun's growth claim with our own arithmetic. Why a
source with an interest cannot settle a question. *Written answers. Pass mark
50%.*

**Level 3 — Can you defend it.** A real Nigerian policy claim you have not
seen. Say how confident you would be and why. Then one final question:

> **State the strongest argument against us, and say what evidence would
> change your mind.**

You cannot pass that by agreeing with us. Somebody who cannot argue against
us has not understood us.

*Written, marked by somebody outside this movement. Pass mark 50%.*

---

## The rules of the exam

**The syllabus is published in advance and free.** Everything on it is in the
book and in the public evidence. Study it without joining, without paying,
without telling anybody who you are.

**The pass mark is 50%, published, fixed before anyone sits.** It is never
adjusted after the scripts are seen. Kaduna's teacher test was attacked for
exactly that — a pass mark of 75% when West African examinations put a credit
at 50% — and the criticism was fair.

**Somebody outside marks Level 3**, and is paid whether you pass or fail.
Otherwise we are marking our own exam.

**We publish pass rates every time, by state.** If only 12% pass, that number
goes up.

**It is free or nearly free to sit**, in English and Pidgin from the first
day, with Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo as soon as native speakers have checked
them. An exam only the comfortable can attempt just rebuilds the elite we are
trying to replace.

**And nothing about your state, religion, ethnic group, party history,
schooling or family appears anywhere in it.** The syllabus is public, the
test is written, the marker is outside. That is the entire filter.

---

## What passing gets you

**It gets you:** the right to speak in this movement's name, to vote in its
decisions, to stand for selection as a candidate, and to be listed publicly
as a member.

**It does not get you:** a position, a contract, a nomination, or a salary.

Passing an exam proves you understand the programme. **It does not prove you
can govern.** Confusing those two is exactly the mistake our own chapter on
leadership warns about.

---

## The things that could go wrong

We would rather name these than have somebody else name them for us.

**It could become a gate for insiders.** Every qualifying body drifts toward
serving its own members. What stops it: free entry, a public syllabus,
outside marking, and pass rates published by state. If the passes cluster in
three states or one class of people, everybody will see it, and we will have
to answer.

**It filters for people who can read and write well.** In a country with real
illiteracy, that is a class filter no matter how carefully we design it. What
we can do: Pidgin and mother-tongue versions, oral examination at Level 1
where needed, and a supporter tier for people who want to be part of this
without sitting a written exam — excluded from speaking in our name, not from
the movement.

**This one is not solved. We are saying so rather than pretending.**

**It will slow us down.** It will. A movement that grows slowly and knows
what it believes beats one that grows fast and does not. But when the
pressure comes to relax the standard for numbers — and it will come — that
pressure must be refused in public, on the record.

**And we might mark our own homework anyway.** The outside examiner is the
safeguard, and an outside examiner we pay is not fully outside. Every working
Nigerian example of independent verification currently has a foreign body
paying the verifier. **We have inherited that problem and have not solved
it.**

---

## One rule that binds us as much as you

The four promises in [Why Join](01_WHY_JOIN.md) bind members too. A member
who states something more confidently than the evidence allows, or attacks a
person instead of an argument, is corrected in public. Repeated, it ends
membership.

**This applies most strictly to whoever leads.** A movement that disciplines
its members and not its founders is one man with a rulebook.
