# What Survives Us

*We ask of every reform in this Blueprint: would it still work under a
mediocre or hostile successor? It would be dishonest not to ask it of
ourselves.*

---

## The test, applied inward

Rule 7 splits any reform in two.

**Stock** — one-time changes that persist by default, because undoing them
costs a fresh painful act.

**Flow** — ongoing practices that die by default at succession unless housed
in something with its own standing and defended by people who lose if it
stops.

Kaduna is our own case study. The teacher dismissals persisted; the testing
practice died the month the governor left. Same state, same handover, and
the open-government machinery survived because it sat in law with an
organised outside constituency.

**So: what in this platform is stock, and what is flow?**

---

## Our stock — what persists even if we all disappear tomorrow

**The published corpus.** Sixteen investigations, eight chapters, eleven
sector briefs, the mistakes file, the red team, the constitutional audit.
Public, versioned, and copyable. Anyone may fork it. Once out, it cannot be
withdrawn — which is exactly why it is published under terms that permit
copying rather than held privately as an asset.

**The qualified members.** Every person who passes the exam has the argument
in their head. That does not expire when an organisation does.

**The findings themselves.** If this platform collapses next year, the
scorekeeper finding remains true, and someone else can use it. **That is a
feature, not a loss.** We would rather the finding outlive the movement than
the movement outlive the finding.

## Our flow — what dies unless deliberately housed

**The investigations.** New nodes, new sector briefs, new corrections. This
is a practice, and practices stop.

**The mistakes file's honesty.** Cheap to maintain now, expensive later. The
moment a reversal is politically costly, the temptation arrives.

**The exam's integrity.** Standards drift. External examiners get replaced
by friendly ones. Pass marks move quietly.

**The refusal to name enemies.** Under attack, this is the first commitment
to break, and the one whose breaking would be most popular internally.

## The five structures that house the flow

Each answers: *what stops this dying when the founder leaves, loses
interest, is bought, or is broken?*

### 1. No claim depends on any person's reputation
Every finding names its source and its status. A reader can check the
platform's claims without trusting the platform. **This is deliberate
protection against our own capture:** if the founder is discredited
tomorrow, the evidence is unaffected, because it never rested on him.

Contrast with the standard Nigerian formation, where the programme *is* the
person, and the programme dies with his reputation.

### 2. The corpus is public and copyable from day one
Not published when convenient. Public now, under terms permitting anyone to
fork, copy, and continue it — **including people who split from us.**

That is uncomfortable and it is the point. A movement that can be
extinguished by capturing its leadership is a weak movement. This one cannot
be, because the thing worth having is already in the open.

### 3. The mistakes file is constitutionally protected
**The rule, written now while it costs nothing:**

> *No entry in the mistakes file may be removed, softened, or made private,
> for any reason, ever — and least of all because it has become politically
> inconvenient.*

We adopt it before we have power precisely because we will not want it once
we do. Anyone may check whether we have kept it: the history is public and
timestamped, so deletion is detectable.

**If this platform ever removes a mistakes entry, that act alone is
sufficient reason to leave it.** We are writing that down while it is easy.

### 4. The exam is governed outside the platform
The external examiner is not a member and is paid whether candidates pass or
fail. Syllabus, pass mark, appeals process and pass rates by state are all
public.

Our own scorekeeper test says an organisation that sets and marks its own
standard will drift. The examiner is the answer, and *it is an imperfect
one* — we pay him, which is the same unresolved dependence the Absorption
Playbook found in every Nigerian verification arrangement. **Recorded as
unsolved.**

### 5. Succession is designed before it is needed
Three provisions, adopted now:

- **The founder holds no permanent office.** Any leadership role carries a
  fixed term, stated in advance.
- **No family succession, ever.** Written now, when it costs nothing.
- **The platform's decisions are recorded and published** the way its
  findings are, so a successor inherits a record rather than a mythology.

## The specific ways this platform will most likely die

Written down so that members can recognise them early, and so nobody can
claim later that it was unforeseeable.

**Death 1 — the founder becomes the product.** Attention concentrates,
"the movement" becomes his name, and the corpus becomes a credential rather
than a working document. *Early warning:* people quote the man more often
than the findings.

**Death 2 — an inconvenient finding gets buried.** The first time evidence
contradicts a political need. *Early warning:* a claim in public that is
stated more confidently than the file behind it.

**Death 3 — the exam softens for growth.** Pressure to expand, pass rates
quietly rise, the standard becomes ceremonial. *Early warning:* pass rates
stop being published, or rise sharply without a syllabus change.

**Death 4 — the enemy frame returns.** Anger recruits faster than evidence.
Under attack, naming enemies will feel like strength. *Early warning:*
platform communications that name a person rather than a mechanism.

**Death 5 — it becomes a consultancy.** Findings get sold rather than
published; the audience becomes donors rather than Nigerians. *Early
warning:* the best work stops appearing in public first.

**Any member may raise any of these publicly at any time, and the platform
must answer in public.** A movement that cannot be criticised by its own
members has already died and not noticed.

---

## The honest limit

Everything above is a rule we wrote for ourselves, and rules written by the
powerful for themselves are the weakest instrument in this entire Blueprint
— we said so about state electoral commissions and about the President's
transmission discretion.

**The only real protection is that the corpus is public and copyable.** If
we break these commitments, the evidence does not break with us: it can be
taken and carried by people who keep them.

**That is the strongest guarantee available, and it is a modest one. We are
not pretending otherwise.**
