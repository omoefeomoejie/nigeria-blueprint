# Node 6 — Decision

**Node:** National Identity
**Question investigated:** Is primary identification in Nigeria responsive to
state performance, and has any comparably diverse country measurably shifted
identification toward the national within a generation, by what mechanism?
**Status:** Decision-grade. **All six backbone nodes now decision-grade.**

---

## The decision

**Ethnic identity in Nigeria is an adaptation, not a fixed inheritance —
fluid, exclusion-responsive, election-activated. The transferable
counter-mechanisms are: (1) contact/intermingling — causally certified
inside Nigeria via NYSC; (2) a group-neutral shared language — which Nigeria
already possesses in Pidgin; (3) fair distribution — routed through Nodes
1/5; (4) elite restraint — an incentive-design problem (shrink the central
pot's activation payoff). Religion is the honest exception: a meaning system
persisting even in success cases; the state's job is preventing capture, not
adjudicating faith. National identity is a flow, never a stock.**

**Confidence: ~70%.** Anchored by the project's strongest causal evidence
(quasi-random assignment, top-journal peer review) but priced for: NYSC
reaches only graduates; attitude/mobility outcomes ≠ wholesale
identification shift; the religion leg rests on the Tanzanian exception,
n=1 deep case.

---

## Evidence ledger

**Certified:**
- Identity fluidity: ethnic self-ID ~50% (2000) → ~33% (2001) → 57% (2003);
  waxes at elections; institutional quality shapes it (Afrobarometer
  longitudinal).
- Exclusion → ethnic retreat: discrimination reduces national-over-ethnic
  prioritisation; strongest among Igbo; 51% believe their group is treated
  unfairly (Afrobarometer R8; peer-reviewed regressions).
- **NYSC contact effect (causal):** Okunogbe 2024, AEJ:Applied 16(1) —
  cross-ethnic posting raised cross-line relocation willingness 15–20pp,
  reduced ethnic-favoritism preferences, effects persisting ~5 years.
- Tanzania mechanism decomposition: neutral language (Swahili belonged to
  no rich group — Ethiopia/Amharic and DRC/Lingala as failed contrasts);
  school intermingling; early fair distribution; elite restraint; plus the
  authoritarian component (chiefdom abolition) named honestly.
- Identity-as-flow: national-identity preference declining across Africa
  R5→R9 (Tanzania −23, Ghana −22, Kenya −20); CCM still campaigning on
  unity 26 years after Nyerere.

**Contested/Partial:**
- Religion as adaptation: Tanzanian Muslim grievances persist despite
  strong national identity — religion only partially reducible to state
  performance.
- Nigerian NYSC literature beyond the AEJ anchor is largely qualitative.

**Preconditions (Rule 5) — Tanzania wholesale transfer FAILS:**
- Nigeria has three dominant groups (Tanzania none), powerful traditional
  institutions (Tanzania abolished — unavailable and undesirable route),
  and an oil pot raising ethnic competition's payoff. Mechanisms transfer;
  the package does not.

**Owed:**
- NYSC effect at non-graduate scale (does any mass-scale contact vehicle
  exist or work?); Pidgin-as-unifier direct evidence; religion-domain
  mechanism beyond capture-prevention.

---

## Recommendation
1. Identity chapter = strengthen the proven machine (NYSC: protect
   cross-ethnic posting integrity, extend contact models below
   graduate level) — not invent a new one.
2. Treat language policy as already-solved-by-the-street: Pidgin is the
   group-neutral lingua franca; the Doctrine's Pidgin rendering is
   nation-building infrastructure, not just distribution.
3. Fair-distribution and elite-restraint legs route through Nodes 1/5
   (shrink the central pot's decisiveness → cut ethnicity's activation
   payoff). Identity policy is partly fiscal policy.
4. Religion: constitutional capture-prevention only; no chapter claims to
   "solve" faith.
5. Rule 7 applies: budget for perpetual maintenance; no identity victory is
   ever banked.
