# Research Habit — Has This Earned the Right to Exist?

Law Zero says no rule enters without a case. This file is the *operational form*
of that law — the questions asked of every proposed addition, and the vocabulary
used to sort them. It is a habit, not a law: a way of thinking that keeps the
engine from accumulating obligations to ideas merely because they were once
proposed by a smart person.

## The three-question test

Every proposed addition must answer all three or it waits:

1. **What problem does this solve?**
2. **Which investigation exposed that problem?**
3. **Could the current engine continue without it?**

If 2 is empty, the idea has no birth certificate. If 3 is "yes," the idea is not
yet necessary. Either way: Candidate, not Earned.

## The three verdicts

- **Earned** — passes all three; a real case paid for it. Implement now.
- **Candidate** — a hypothesis about what the engine might later need. **Not on a
  roadmap. Carries no obligation to ship.** Reality may disprove it before it is
  ever earned. It waits without a promise.
- **Rejected** — investigated and found unnecessary or wrong. Logged, not deleted.

The word *Candidate* is deliberate. "Staged" implies arrival; "Candidate" implies
nothing but possibility. The engine owes its candidates nothing.

## The four kinds of change (so unlike things aren't compared)

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Patch** | Clarification or correction of existing content | Separating Facts from Interpretations |
| **Rule** | A new constraint within a skill | "Beware confounded cases" |
| **Capability** | A whole new skill | Comparative Statecraft Engine |
| **Governance** | A law or standard above the skills | Law Zero; "Rejected" status |

These are different species. A future debate that compares a patch to a capability
as if they were the same kind of decision is a confused debate. Name the type
first.
