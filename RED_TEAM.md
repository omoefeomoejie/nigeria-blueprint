# Red Team

*Written as an opponent, not as an author. Every weakness below is ranked by
how much damage it does if a hostile analyst finds it before we admit it.*

*Method note: the project has falsified itself node by node, in the moment,
with the instruments it had at the time. This is the first pass over the
finished body of work as a whole, using the sharper instruments we now hold —
absorption, who-keeps-the-score, stock/flow, route-matching, the
flagship-case trap.*

---

## Tier 1 — Would hurt badly

### 1.1 Every claim was verified by one entity, against indexed sources
No human source has been consulted. No revenue chairman, no SUBEB officer,
no procurement director, no farmer. Everything here is desk research by a
single reader with no field access and no adversarial reviewer.

**The attack:** "You have never spoken to anyone who does this work."
**Our answer:** true, and stated. That is why every claim carries a status
and a named owed fact. But it remains the single largest structural weakness
in the corpus and no amount of additional searching fixes it.

### 1.2 Chapter 2 recommends what Chapter 2 admits has never worked
The leadership chapter proposes merit selection as the mechanism for a
meritocratic pipeline, while stating that no Nigerian case has shown it
improved services or survived its founder.

**The attack:** "Your own chapter says this has never produced an outcome.
You are recommending an unproven intervention."
**Our answer:** the chapter says so in its opening paragraph rather than
burying it, and the design responds to both gaps. But a critic quoting our
own admission back at us has an easy hit, and Node 2 at ~60% is the weakest
load-bearing node in the book.

### 1.3 The units-first thesis has one measured boundary and may have more
We found that units-first fails where the unit keeps its own score
(elections). That was discovered late, by accident, in the eighth node.

**The attack:** "You found one exception by luck. How many more are there?"
**Our answer:** honest — we do not know. The who-keeps-the-score test now
exists but has been applied retrospectively to only one domain. **Owed:
run that test against all eight nodes deliberately.** This is the single
most useful piece of unfinished work the red team found.

### 1.4 Enugu carries three chapters and has a debt problem
Enugu is the revenue proof (Ch.1), a transparency laggard (Ch.4), and an
early electricity-transfer state (Sector 02). Its debt more than doubled
while it built its revenue story, and its 2024 audited accounts are
unpublished.

**The attack:** "Your flagship state borrowed its way there and won't show
its books."
**Our answer:** all three facts are in the corpus, added by our own audit.
But the concentration is a real fragility: if Enugu's audit reveals
something bad, three chapters move at once.

## Tier 2 — Would need a careful answer

### 2.1 The absorption playbook rests partly on self-reported numbers
UBEC's 95% procurement completion, 8.7m learners and ₦100bn mobilised are
UBEC describing UBEC. The independent anchor is Tracka's 78% ranking, which
corroborates direction, not magnitude.

**Our answer:** the split is stated in the playbook. But six "practices"
extracted from an organisation's self-description are hypotheses, and we
called them practices.

### 2.2 Pay-on-verified-output has no fully domestic instance
Every working Nigerian case carries an externally funded verifier. We
recommend the mechanism anyway.

**Our answer:** stated, with a plural-verification workaround proposed. The
workaround is untested and we invented it.

### 2.3 The interdependence map is inference stacked on inference
Individual dependencies are documented. The ranking is not. It changed three
times in three commits.

**Our answer:** marked as inference in the file, and the changes were
corrections we published. But a critic can reasonably say the sequence is
our opinion wearing evidence's clothes.

### 2.4 We use BudgIT and Tracka heavily
A large share of the fiscal and project evidence comes from one civic
organisation and its platform.

**The attack:** "Your independent verification is one NGO."
**Our answer:** BudgIT is independent of government and its methods are
public, which is why we lean on it. But source concentration is real, and we
have not sought a second civic auditor to triangulate.

### 2.5 The Doctrine's Wager is untested and load-bearing for Part V
The whole movement layer rests on a folk model.

**Our answer:** labelled Untested everywhere it appears, and Part V calls
itself the weakest part. Honest, and still a hole.

## Tier 3 — Fair hits we should absorb

### 3.1 Recency bias toward 2024–26 sources
Most evidence is recent. Longer time series would test whether findings are
structural or cyclical — especially the revenue growth cases, which coincide
with subsidy removal and devaluation inflating nominal figures.

### 3.2 Nominal naira figures across a devaluation
Several headline numbers (IGR growth especially) are nominal, in a period
when the naira moved sharply. Real-terms restatement is owed and would
reduce some of the growth we celebrate.

### 3.3 Southern and northern coverage is uneven
Revenue cases skew south and central (Lagos, Enugu, Osun, Kwara, Kaduna).
Security and agriculture cases skew north. Neither axis was designed; both
followed the data.

### 3.4 No costing anywhere
Not one chapter states what its pilot costs. A commissioner would ask that
question first.
