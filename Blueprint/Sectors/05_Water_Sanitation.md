# Water & Sanitation

*Three quarters of Nigeria's water points are self-supplied. That single
statistic is this Blueprint's diagnosis completed at household level: when
the state fails, people exit rather than demand — and the exit dissolves the
constituency that would have forced delivery.*

---

## 1. What the sector produces now

Nigeria has about **2.31 million water points**, of which **75% are
self-supplied** and 43% are boreholes [Certified — Federal Ministry of Water
Resources].

Public water utilities produce water in **28 of 36 states**, but are
considered *functional* — consistently producing, supplying active
consumers, and collecting user fees — in only **16** [Certified].

Service level: average **9 litres per person per day**, against a national
minimum standard of 12–16 litres. One-third of Nigerians drink contaminated
water at home. **46 million people practise open defecation** — 23% of the
population [Certified — WASHNORM / UNICEF].

**40% of boreholes fail within two years** [Certified].

A 2019 Executive Order committed Nigeria to ending open defecation by 2025.
That deadline has passed unmet.

## 2. What is broken

**The state built and then lost the assets.** Losing 40% of boreholes inside
two years is a maintenance failure, not a construction failure. Nigeria is
not short of drilled holes; it is short of working ones.

**Utilities that cannot bill cannot survive.** Only 16 of 36 states run
utilities that actually collect user fees [Certified]. A utility that
produces water but collects nothing is a subsidy programme with pipes, and
it degrades until it stops.

**Citizens have exited.** 75% self-supply. Every household that drills its
own borehole is a household that will never again lobby for municipal water
— and they are disproportionately the households with the loudest voices.
The sector's political constituency has been quietly bought out, one
borehole at a time.

**Targets without measurement discipline.** The 2025 open-defecation
deadline passed with 46 million people still practising it. The sector
measures well (WASHNORM is genuinely good data) but the targets were never
tied to consequences.

**Fifth convergence — and the third instance of absorptive capacity.**
Power: 16 states took electricity oversight they could not operate.
Education: 27 of 36 states could not draw down money already theirs. Water:
utilities exist in 28 states and function in 16. Three sectors, three
different resources — authority, money, infrastructure — one constraint:
**the state's ability to absorb and operate what it already has.** Promoted
from candidate to stated finding in Part II, Layer 3.

## 4. What is genuinely sector-specific

**Exit is available here, and that changes the politics.** You cannot
privately drill your own police force or your own currency. You can drill
your own borehole for a few hundred thousand naira. Sectors where exit is
cheap lose their reform constituency first, which is why water reform is
politically invisible despite affecting everyone. Any water strategy must
therefore *recruit* the exited, not assume them.

**Community maintenance is the sector's own named fix.** WASH committees,
community ownership, active participation — the sector keeps returning to
this because facilities without an owner decay [Certified]. It is Rule 7
discovered independently by water engineers.

**Sanitation is behavioural, not only infrastructural.** Open defecation
persists in places with toilets. This is the one sector output in this
Blueprint that requires changing what people *do*, not only what the state
builds.

## 5. What one state could try in 18 months

Powers required: state water agency powers. Already held.

1. **Publish a functionality register, not an asset register.** Every water
   point: working / not working / last maintained. Nigeria counts what it
   built; almost nobody publishes what still runs.
2. **Fix billing before building.** A utility that collects fees can
   maintain; one that cannot will lose the next 40% too. Meter and bill the
   connections you already have before drilling anything new.
3. **Fund maintenance as a named budget line** with its own published
   figure, and attach every new facility to a maintenance contract before
   commissioning.
4. **Give every facility an owner** — a WASH committee with a legal
   relationship to the asset, not a launch ceremony (Rule 7).
5. **Recruit the exited.** Target the first improvements at areas dense with
   self-supply, because those households are the constituency the sector
   lost, and they are the ones who can make noise when service fails.

**Kill condition.** If after 18 months the functionality rate has not risen
even though new points were built, stop building entirely and put the whole
budget into maintenance and billing. The evidence says construction is not
the constraint.
