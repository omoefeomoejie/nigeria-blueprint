# Node 7 — Decision

**Node:** Skills & Education
**Question investigated:** Has any poor, low-capacity state or country
measurably raised *learning* (not enrolment) within a decade, and by what
mechanism?
**Status:** Decision-grade. Closes the gap Vision 2050 flagged as
un-investigated.

---

## The decision

**Learning can be raised measurably in a poor subnational unit, and the
proven mechanism is fiscal, not pedagogical: tie a share of the state's
discretionary transfers to municipal learning outcomes, supply technical
assistance, and measure continuously. The Blueprint's education chapter is
executable by one willing governor with no constitutional amendment — and
it must be designed for equity explicitly, because the default rewards the
already-strong.**

**Confidence: ~72%.** High on the Ceará mechanism (border-discontinuity
identification, World Bank replication guide, structurally analogous to a
Nigerian state). Priced down because: the flagship international case
(Vietnam) is Confounded; no Nigerian instance exists; and Nigeria lacks the
monitoring precondition.

---

## Evidence ledger

**Certified (independent, causal):**
- Ceará RBF: ninth-graders exposed to results-based financing scored
  **0.15 SD** higher in maths and language (schools compared across the
  state border); impacts **double** where technical assistance was added;
  significant for fifth-graders too (Lautharte, de Oliveira & Loureiro,
  World Bank PRWP 9509, 2021).
- Design: 25% of ICMS goes to municipalities by constitution; the state
  controls a quarter of that; Ceará assigned **18% to an Education Quality
  Index** (grade-2 literacy, grade-5 reading/maths, grade 1–5 pass rates).
- Transfers are **general-purpose** — mayors may spend anywhere — which
  recruits the whole municipal government, not only education officials.
- Outcome: fifth-poorest of 26 Brazilian states; largest IDEB growth since
  2005; ten municipalities in the national top 20.
- **Mechanism is attention, not money:** municipalities receiving more ICMS
  did NOT raise education spending (~R$0.45/real to education; total
  per-capita spending +R$1.96). Accountability, not budget size, moved
  learning.
- Equity is a design parameter: gains concentrated in top quantiles until
  the rule was reformulated to penalise municipalities with more low
  performers — which then significantly reduced learning gaps.

**Confounded:**
- **Vietnam** — outperforms all developing countries on PISA and remains a
  positive outlier after correcting for out-of-school bias; BUT child-,
  household- and school-level variables explain **at most ~30%** of the
  performance, most of it *household* inputs rather than policy levers.
  Growth and culture are entangled with reform. Cannot carry a chapter.
  (Also: OECD could not fully validate its 2018 data; classroom practice
  remains rote; Kinh/minority equity gaps persist.)

**Preconditions (Rule 5):** a decentralised school system (Nigeria HAS —
basic education is state/LG) and a robust monitoring & evaluation system
(Nigeria LACKS — this is the pilot's first build).

**Owed:**
- Any Nigerian instance of learning-linked transfers.
- Nigerian baseline learning data of sufficient quality to compute an index.

---

## Recommendation
1. Education chapter = **Ceará transplant**: a state ties a defined share
   of its discretionary transfers to an LG Education Quality Index, adds
   technical assistance (the impact-doubler), and builds the assessment
   system first.
2. Equity clause from day one (penalise low-performer concentration) —
   otherwise the mechanism widens gaps.
3. Do not build the chapter on Vietnam; cite it only as existence proof
   that poverty is not destiny.
4. Cross-node fusion: this is a **fiscal** lever, so Chapters 1 and 7 share
   machinery (traceability, published indices) — as does Node 4's
   measurement discipline. Third cross-domain appearance of
   measurement-plus-incentive.
