# What Survives Us

*Our book asks one question of every reform in Nigeria: would this still work
under a bad successor? It would be dishonest not to ask it of ourselves.*

---

## The test, turned inward

Kaduna is our own warning.

The teacher testing died the month the governor left. The contracts portal,
in the same state, at the same handover, survived and grew.

The difference was not which reform was better. **One was written into law
with people outside who would shout if it stopped. The other lived in one
man's will.**

So: what part of this movement is written down and copyable, and what part
lives in somebody's energy?

---

## What survives even if we all disappear

**The published work.** Sixteen investigations, twenty-six chapters, twelve
sector briefs, the mistakes file, the document where we attack ourselves. All
public. All copyable.

**Anyone may take it.** Including people who leave us. Including people who
split from us and think we are wrong.

That is uncomfortable and it is deliberate. **A movement that can be killed
by capturing its leadership is a weak movement.** This one cannot be, because
the thing worth having is already outside the building.

**The people who passed the exam.** Every one of them has the argument in
their head. That does not expire when an organisation does.

**And the findings themselves.** If this movement collapses next year, it
will still be true that UBEC finishes 78% of its projects and the Niger Delta
ministry finishes 16. Somebody else can use that.

**We would rather the findings outlive the movement than the movement outlive
the findings.**

---

## What dies unless we hold it deliberately

**The investigating.** New work, new corrections. That is a practice, and
practices stop.

**The honesty about mistakes.** Cheap now. Expensive the day a reversal costs
us politically.

**The standard of the exam.** Standards drift. Outside examiners get replaced
by friendly ones. Pass marks move quietly.

**And the refusal to name enemies.** Under attack, that will be the first
thing people want to abandon, and abandoning it will be popular inside the
room.

---

## The five things that hold them

**1. Nothing we claim depends on anybody's reputation.**

Every finding names its source. You can check us without trusting us. Which
means: **if whoever leads this is discredited tomorrow, the evidence is
unaffected**, because it never rested on him.

That is the opposite of how Nigerian movements usually work, where the
programme *is* the man, and dies with his reputation.

**2. Everything is public and copyable from day one.**

Not published when convenient. Public now.

**3. The mistakes file is protected by a written rule.**

> *No entry in the mistakes file may be removed, softened, or made private,
> for any reason, ever — and least of all because it has become politically
> inconvenient.*

Adopted before we have anything to lose, because we know we will not want it
later. The history is public and dated, so deletion can be seen.

**If this movement ever removes an entry, that act alone is sufficient reason
to leave it.** We are writing that down while it is easy.

**4. The exam is governed from outside.**

Outside examiner, not a member, paid whether people pass or fail. Public
syllabus, public pass mark, published pass rates by state.

**And it is imperfect** — we pay him, which is the same problem every
Nigerian verification arrangement has. Recorded, unsolved.

**5. Succession is designed before anybody needs it.**

Nobody holds a permanent office. Any leadership role has a fixed term, stated
in advance. **No family succession, ever** — written now, when it costs
nothing to write. And decisions are recorded and published the way findings
are, so whoever comes next inherits a record rather than a legend.

---

## The five ways this dies

Written down so members can spot them early, and so nobody can say later that
it could not have been foreseen.

**The founder becomes the product.** Attention gathers around one man, the
movement takes his name, and the work becomes his credential.
*Watch for:* people quoting the man more than the findings.

**An inconvenient finding gets buried.** The first time evidence contradicts
something politically useful.
*Watch for:* a claim made in public that is more confident than the file
behind it.

**The exam softens for growth.** Pressure to expand, pass rates quietly rise,
the standard becomes ceremony.
*Watch for:* pass rates no longer published, or rising sharply without the
syllabus changing.

**The enemy frame comes back.** Anger recruits faster than evidence, and
under attack, naming enemies will feel like courage.
*Watch for:* anything from us that names a person rather than a mechanism.

**It becomes a consultancy.** Findings sold instead of published, donors
replacing Nigerians as the audience.
*Watch for:* the best work no longer appearing in public first.

**Any member may raise any of these publicly at any time, and we must answer
in public.** A movement that cannot be criticised by its own members has
already died and not noticed.

---

## The honest limit

Everything above is a rule we wrote for ourselves. And rules written by the
powerful, for themselves, are the weakest instrument in our entire book — we
said exactly that about state electoral commissions, and about a President
deciding when to send a proposal forward.

**The only real protection is that the work is public and copyable.**

If we break these promises, the evidence does not break with us. It can be
picked up and carried by people who keep them.

**That is the strongest guarantee available, and it is a modest one. We are
not pretending otherwise.**
