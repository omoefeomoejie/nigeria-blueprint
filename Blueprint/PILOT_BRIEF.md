# Two Things You Can Start This Year

*For a governor, a commissioner, or anyone who would have to actually do this.
Both use powers you already hold. Neither needs Abuja's permission or a
constitutional amendment. Both have been done, by named states, with numbers
you can check.*

---

## Pilot One: Money that comes in, and shows

**What it is.** Three changes, in this order.

Kill cash collection everywhere in the state. Every MDA, no exceptions, six
months. Enugu did this and its revenue went from ₦37.6bn to ₦180.5bn in a
year. The point is not the money; it is that cash cannot be traced and
therefore cannot be counted.

Harmonise your taxes. One schedule, published, ending double and triple
collection on the same business. Osun did this and its revenue rose 162%
between 2021 and 2025, confirmed by an independent civic audit. This is the
easiest of the three politically, because businesses become your allies
instead of your victims. Start here if you want an early win.

Publish what the money built. Quarterly: naira collected, projects
delivered. Enugu moved from a recurrent-heavy budget to roughly 79–86%
capital spending. Most states collect more and deliver the same. That gap is
where public trust dies.

**Why it holds after you leave.** Put the revenue service in law, not in a
circular. Kwara's revenue service was created by state law in 2015, survived
a change of government in 2019 that swept out almost everything else, and
the state is now third nationally on independent fiscal rankings. A revenue
agency that exists only because you said so will end when you do.

**What it costs you.** The people who live on cash collection will fight, and
some of them work for you. That is the whole cost. There is no technical
obstacle.

**Numbers to watch.** Year one: no cash collection anywhere, and a published
leakage baseline. Year three: IGR up 50% on baseline, capital spending above
55%. If revenue rises and nothing gets built, stop and fix delivery first.

## Pilot Two: Paying local governments for learning

**What it is.** You already send transfers to your local governments. Tie a
share of them to how much children in those LGs actually learn.

Ceará is the fifth-poorest state in Brazil. It assigned 18% of its
municipal transfer quota to an education index measuring whether children
can read by primary two and do arithmetic by primary five. Students in
schools exposed to this scored measurably higher than students just across
the state border. Where the state also sent technical help, the effect
doubled. Ceará went on to record the largest education improvement in
Brazil.

**The part that surprises people.** The local governments that got more money
did not spend it on education. Roughly 45 kobo in the naira went to schools.
The learning improved anyway, because mayors started paying attention to
something they were being measured on. This is not a spending programme. It
is an attention programme.

**How you start.** Build the test first. An independent annual assessment of
primary two literacy and primary five numeracy in every LG, published in
full. Most states have never published such a number. Then gazette the
formula. Then send technical help to the worst-performing quarter, because
that is what doubles the effect.

**One thing you must not skip.** Ceará's early gains landed in schools that
were already strong. They fixed it by penalising LGs with many low
performers. Put that clause in from day one or you will widen the gap you
are trying to close.

**Numbers to watch.** Year one: baseline published, formula gazetted. Year
three: measurable improvement, with the bottom quarter of LGs rising at
least as fast as the top. If only the strong schools improve, or if schools
start keeping weak pupils away from the test, stop and change the rule.

## Why these two

They are the only chapters in this Blueprint that a state can execute alone,
in full, starting now. Everything else needs either the National Assembly,
the courts, or federal cooperation.

They also reinforce each other. Pilot one gives you money you can trace.
Pilot two gives you something to point at when people ask what the money
bought.

---

## What we ask in return

Publish the numbers, including the bad ones. The whole argument of this
Blueprint is that Nigerian government can be checked. A pilot that succeeds
quietly proves nothing to anybody.
