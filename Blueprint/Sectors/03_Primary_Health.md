# Primary Health

*The sector that built the measuring instrument our chapters keep asking
for — and then published a number that embarrassed its own programme. That
is the most encouraging thing in this brief.*

---

## 1. What the sector produces now

Nigeria has roughly **17,600 political wards**, many historically without a
properly equipped health facility. In 2023 the country committed to
revitalising **17,600 PHCs in four years** — one functional centre per ward
[Certified — NPHCDA].

Progress as reported by the agency itself: **1,163 PHCs fully revitalised**
by mid-2025, with 2,774 more in various stages of upgrade. The public
dashboard listed **1,295 revitalised as of 20 September 2025 — 7.3% of the
four-year target** [Certified — Nigeria Health Watch, computing from
NPHCDA's own data].

For context on volume: over 20 million patient visits to PHCs per quarter
[Certified — NPHCDA].

## 2. What is broken

**Ambition and delivery are an order of magnitude apart.** 7.3% at the
halfway mark of a four-year target. And there is precedent: a 2016–17
national pledge to revitalise 10,000 PHCs "was not realised by the end of
that administration" [Certified].

**"Revitalised" is doing unexamined work.** The honest question, put by the
sector's own watchdog: does revitalisation mean a fresh coat of paint, or
meaningful improvement in the quality of care? [Certified]. Field reporting
gives both answers in the same state — Kaduna's Narayi PHC shows genuine
gains in staffing, services and 24-hour delivery, while a companion
investigation is titled *"Fresh paint, old problems"* and a separate
renovated facility is being destroyed by erosion [Certified].

**Money does not arrive predictably at the frontline.** Resources often fail
to reach frontline facilities in a predictable way, undermining trust in the
system [Certified].

**The hard constraint is people and utilities, not buildings.** A fully
functional Level 2 PHC must provide 24-hour service, safe delivery,
antenatal care, immunisation, laboratory and pharmacy services, reliable
electricity, water and adequate staffing — with at least four skilled birth
attendants per functional PHC [Certified]. Any of those missing and the
building is a building.

## 3. Which horizontal failure explains each part

| Broken part | Horizontal failure | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Refurbished but not delivering care | Execution/conversion — collection is not delivery | Ch. 1 |
| Funds not reaching the frontline predictably | Traceability absent at the last mile | Ch. 4 |
| Pledges dying with administrations (2016–17, and the risk now) | Flow without statutory housing | Rule 7, Ch. 2 |
| Staffing gaps | Selection and posting | Ch. 2 |
| No power or water at the facility | Another sector's failure landing here | Sector 02 |

**Third convergence** — and the most useful variant so far. Health did not
merely restate the diagnosis; it demonstrated the cure. NPHCDA made its
monitoring dashboard **publicly accessible in August 2025**, putting
granular data on infrastructure, functionality and services up for scrutiny
for the first time. Within weeks an independent organisation used that data
to publish the 7.3% figure [Certified].

That is Chapter 4's chain — publication → usability → scrutiny — firing in
a real Nigerian sector, *against the publishing agency's own interest.*
Health is further along the accountability chain than revenue is. The
Blueprint should say so, and borrow from it rather than lecture it.

## 4. What is genuinely sector-specific

**The definition of success is the reform's hardest problem.** In revenue,
"₦ collected" is unambiguous. Here, "functional" is a composite of staffing,
utilities, drugs and hours — and every component can be present while care
is still absent. Sectors whose output is a *service* need a definition
before they need a budget.

**A statutorily earmarked funding flow already exists.** The Basic Health
Care Provision Fund draws not less than **1% of the Consolidated Revenue
Fund**, with an increase to 2% proposed [Certified]. This is Rule 7's
prescription — a flow housed in statute rather than in a circular — already
implemented in this one sector. No other sector in this Blueprint has it.
Study it before designing anything new.

**Facilities decay faster than they are built.** Erosion destroying a
renovated PHC is not a metaphor; maintenance is a recurring cost the
revitalisation model has not priced [Certified].

## 5. What one state could try in 18 months

Powers required: state and LG health powers. Already held.

1. **Audit function, not fabric.** Take the NPHCDA dashboard's list for your
   state and verify each "revitalised" facility against the functional
   definition: 24-hour service, four skilled birth attendants, power, water,
   drugs. Publish the pass rate. Nobody has done this at state level.
2. **Sequence staffing and utilities before construction.** A staffed,
   powered, watered old building beats a painted empty new one. This
   reverses the default order.
3. **Trace the money to the facility**, not to the LGA — the BHCPF financial
   management app already piloted in Rivers, Ekiti, Gombe and Kaduna is the
   existing instrument [Certified]; adopt it rather than build one.
4. **Price maintenance into every project** and publish the maintenance line
   separately.
5. **Publish quarterly:** facilities meeting the full functional definition,
   as a share of wards.

**Kill condition.** If after 18 months the number of *refurbished* facilities
has risen but the number meeting the full functional definition has not,
stop building and move the entire budget to staffing and utilities. Say so
publicly, with the numbers.
