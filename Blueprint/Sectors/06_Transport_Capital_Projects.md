# Transport & Capital Projects

*One federal agency completes 78% of its projects. Another completes 16%.
Same country, same contractors, same law, same year. The failure everyone
calls "Nigerian" is organisational — diagnosable, rankable, fixable.*

---

## 1. What the sector produces now

**The contested headline.** By end-September 2025, capital expenditure stood
at **₦3.10 trillion against ₦17.58 trillion budgeted — 17.66%** [Certified —
Budget Office of the Federation, 2025 Q3 implementation report]. For every
₦100 allocated to infrastructure, ₦17.66 was spent.

**The government's rebuttal, recorded in full.** The Finance Ministry states
total capital expenditure reached **₦11.59tn in 2024 (≈84% performance)** and
about **₦11.7tn in 2025 (≈76%)**, arguing that MDA cash-release figures alone
mislead because project-tied financing from development partners bypasses
federal accounts [Certified — Ministry of Finance].

**How to read the conflict [Rule 3, Rule 8].** Both figures are real and
measure different things: one tracks disbursement through the federal
channel, the other total capital spend across all channels. Neither settles
the question alone. What is *not* disputed by either side: **₦5.71 trillion
of unfinished 2025 projects was rolled into the 2026 budget**, and a former
Director-General of the Budget Office puts abandoned projects nationwide at
**over 56,000** [Certified].

Also undisputed: the federal government borrowed **₦1.54 trillion more than
projected** in the first nine months of 2025 [Certified].

## 2. What is broken

**Money is released and nothing is delivered.** BudgIT's Tracka assessed
**2,760 federal projects across 30 states** and found projects where funds
were released and contractors paid, yet nothing was built — ₦7.8bn of the
₦8bn tied to abandoned-but-paid projects. Tracka's head states the finding
plainly: *"This is no longer a funding problem… That points clearly to
failures after disbursement"* [Certified].

**Borrowing runs ahead of the ability to spend.** Over-borrowing by ₦1.54tn
while leaving the capital budget largely unexecuted is not a liquidity
problem. It is an absorption problem carrying interest.

**The rollover trap.** Carrying ₦5.71tn of unfinished work forward
normalises a cycle in which budgets are passed, funds allocated, and
infrastructure is not built — and the fiscal-year extension has itself
become a fixture of Nigerian budget management [Certified].

**Awarding outruns finishing.** In the roads sub-sector, contracts have been
terminated and withdrawn for non-delivery while new contracts continue to be
awarded, with some roads unbuilt for decades [Certified — Ministry of Works
statements, 2024–25].

## 3. Which horizontal failure explains each part

| Broken part | Horizontal failure | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Paid-for projects undelivered | Execution/conversion — the layer Node 1 called rarest | Ch. 1 |
| No consequence after disbursement | Accountability chain broken at enforcement | Ch. 4 |
| Rollovers normalised across years | Flow without housing; no succession discipline | Rule 7 |
| Contracts awarded without funding certainty | Procurement integrity | Ch. 4 |

**Fourth instance of absorptive capacity — and its first measured
distribution.** Power (authority), education (money), water (infrastructure),
now capital projects (execution). But this sector adds what the others could
not: **97.5% of abandoned-but-paid projects sit in five states — Taraba,
Abia, Nasarawa, Adamawa, Ogun — while seventeen states recorded none**
[Certified — Tracka, 2,760 projects, 30 states].

Absorption is therefore **not a national trait but a variable** — and the
sharpest cut is organisational rather than geographic. Tracka's agency
ranking: **UBEC 78% completion, Federal Ministry of Agriculture 68%, Rural
Electrification Agency 65%; Lower Benue River Basin Development Authority
20%, Ministry of Niger Delta Affairs 16%** [Certified]. A 62-point spread
inside one government.

**Honest correction:** an earlier draft of this brief said "seventeen states
recorded none." The fuller data is narrower — **Cross River** alone recorded
no abandoned, unexecuted or fraudulently delivered projects; top-ten
completion rates are only ~52–63%; and *non-execution* concentrates in a
different five states (Benue 40%, Ondo 32.4%, Kwara 30.4%, Akwa Ibom 27.3%,
Sokoto 25.6%). Nationally: 52.10% completed, 23.91% ongoing, ~24%
abandoned/not executed/fraudulently delivered.

**Mechanism candidate:** Tracka's own explanation — *"Because there are no
consequences for abandonment… it is almost a tradition for local officials
to just decide to start another project"* — makes this an incentive problem
before a competence problem.

## 4. What is genuinely sector-specific

**Capital projects fail *after* the money moves, which is unusual.** In
education the money never leaves; in water the asset decays slowly. Here,
funds are disbursed, contractors are paid, and delivery simply does not
occur — a distinct failure mode requiring post-disbursement enforcement, not
better budgeting.

**Independent tracking already exists and works.** Tracka physically visits
projects and publishes findings. Unlike most sectors in this Blueprint, the
measurement instrument is built, running, and civil-society-owned. The gap
is consequence, not information.

**A live federal fix is being attempted.** A new rule barring MDAs from
awarding contracts without secured funding is intended to cut mobilisation-
before-funding and reduce the abandoned stock [Certified, 2026]. **Watch
item:** whether it changes the 2026–27 numbers. If it does, it is evidence
that procurement rules can move absorption.

## 5. What one state could try in 18 months

Powers required: state capital-budget and procurement powers. Already held.

1. **Publish a project register with physical status** — every capital
   project, amount released, contractor, percentage complete, verified by
   photograph. Tracka's method, run by the state on itself.
2. **Stop new awards until the existing stock is resolved.** Finish, or
   formally terminate and recover. A state that awards while abandoning is
   buying its own rollover trap.
3. **Tie payment tranches to verified physical progress**, not to elapsed
   time or paperwork.
4. **Adopt funding-certainty rules locally** — no award without secured
   funds — mirroring the federal 2026 directive.
5. **Publish the completion rate quarterly** and rank your own MDAs.

**Kill condition.** If after 18 months the completion rate has not risen
despite full disbursement, the constraint is contractor capacity or
collusion rather than state administration — shift to prequalification
reform and open-competition rules (Chapter 4) rather than more monitoring.
